You are helping the user evaluate and strengthen **Guiding Decisions** — the Design Assessment dimension that measures whether signals create direction or get lost in debate.

## Core idea

Most teams have more input than they can act on — users, stakeholders, leadership, sales, support, research, analytics, market. The problem is not that teams lack information. It is that information often does not turn into a decision. Meetings circle. Evidence gets discussed but not used. The same decisions keep coming back.

Guiding Decisions is about matching the **kind of decision** to the **kind of reasoning** the moment needs. The dimension names four decision types — **Exploratory, Operational, Analytical, Evidential** — that sit across two tensions: *Effectual ↔ Causal* (start from what you have, or work backward from a goal) and *Abstract ↔ Certain* (still shaping, or ready to commit).

Strong teams know how to use the right signal at the right moment. Weak teams force every decision through the same process — over-planning early ideas, under-testing risky choices, or staying exploratory when it is time to commit.

## How to apply

1. **Confirm this is the right dimension.** Symptoms include: meetings ending without a clear decision, teams asking for more data without defining what would be enough, findings shared but not connected to tradeoffs, options compared loosely, decisions depending on hierarchy instead of evidence, the same issue resurfacing in later reviews, action items without real commitment. If the symptom is more about lost learning, complexity mismatch, weak proof, or local-only proof, route to a different dimension.

2. **Name the decision before debating the solution.** The single highest-leverage move. Most stuck decisions are stuck because nobody named what decision was actually being made.

3. **Match decision type to reasoning type:**
   - **Exploratory** (early, abstract, what we have) → needs *shaping*, not certainty. Capture assumptions and hunches, make the early direction clear enough to test.
   - **Operational** (goal-directed, structured) → needs *structure*, not more loose discussion. Connect goals to methods, ownership, workflows.
   - **Analytical** (evidence-seeking, comparing) → needs *interpretation*, not more scattered input. Use UX metrics and findings to understand what changed.
   - **Evidential** (high-commitment, ready to choose) → needs *commitment*, not more circling. State what was chosen, what was rejected, why it matters, what's next.

4. **Push toward the decision trail.** `Uncertainty → Options → Signal → Choice` is the trail every decision should leave. If the team can't reconstruct it for one recent decision, that's where to start.

5. **Separate opinion from evidence explicitly.** A common failure pattern is treating all input as equal. Strong Guiding Decisions makes the difference visible in the conversation.

6. **For evaluating a single review meeting, route to `glare-design-review`.** That's a different unit of analysis — one conversation vs. the system. Guiding Decisions is the dimension; Design Review is one place where it gets tested.
