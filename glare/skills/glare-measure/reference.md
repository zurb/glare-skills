# Measure — Compressed

## What this block is about

Measure is the second facet of the Glare framework (a Helio methodology for cutting through the "Fog" of design indecision). It is the discipline of turning intuition into evidence-backed design signals so ideas earn clarity quickly instead of dying in meetings. Measure operates through four sequential moves — **Concepts → Hunches → Questioning → Findings** — that take big initiatives, break them into testable bets, sharpen them with neutral questions tied to UX metrics, and convert raw data into actionable signals tied to both user needs and business goals. Data alone is noise; Measure is how teams produce clarity that leaders can trust and act on in the moment.

---

## When to use

- When the user is producing evidence within the Decision Map's Measure area.
- When forming hypotheses or hunches, writing research questions, running tests, or interpreting findings.
- When working the four-move loop — Concepts → Hunches → Questioning → Findings.
- When using UX metric families (Behavioral / Attitudinal / Performance / Perceptual).
- When the team has hunches but no falsifiable bets, or data but no signals.

**Don't use when** still defining who/what to study (use `glare-define`) or when there are results and the team needs to compare versions (`glare-focus`). For depth in one move, use `glare-measure-concepts`, `glare-measure-hunches`, `glare-measure-questioning`, or `glare-measure-findings`.

## Glare | Measuring Overview v1.0

The high-level introduction to the Measure facet. Establishes the "Fog vs. Signal" framing and the four-move loop.

- Ideas collapse not because they're bad but because proof comes too late; Measure cuts through by producing evidence in the moment.
- Measure makes design intent visible, ties it to real user problems, and shows whether work delivers value leaders can trust.
- Every concept demands its own lens (onboarding ≠ ad ≠ app), but Glare holds consistency across projects so teams can compare fairly.
- The four moves of Measure:
  - **Concepts** → Define clear goals connecting design work to user and business challenges. Without them, effort scatters.
  - **Hunches** → Start with informed guesses. They spark exploration and give signals something to confirm or kill.
  - **Questioning** → Ask sharper questions that uncover what matters. Right framing turns noise into usable answers.
  - **Findings** → Translate raw data into signals. Each one reduces doubt and keeps ideas alive long enough to prove value.
- Per-move call-outs:
  - *Concepts*: anchor in real user problems; focus on one challenge at a time; align teams around shared goals; decide which signals matter most.
  - *Hunches*: treat as testable hypotheses; act without waiting for perfect data; spark creative exploration; balance instinct with signals as proof.
  - *Questioning*: avoid biased framing; tie questions to outcomes; uncover the "why" behind behavior; refine each round.
  - *Findings*: spot patterns that reveal blockers; translate data into actionable signals; align user and business needs; share to build trust.
- Reference reading: Teresa Torres (outputs vs. outcomes), Christina Wodtke (instinct for metrics), Julie Zhuo (building with data), Jeff Patton (effort & outcome visibility), Tomer Sharon (user happiness), John Cutler (feature factory signs), Laura Klein (product decisions), Jared Spool (design metrics that matter).

## Glare | Measure, Concepts v1.0

Concepts are the first move: focused design efforts tied to user needs and business priorities. They anchor intent before solutions.

- **Why concepts matter**: Stay aligned (link work to measurable user/business outcomes); Make success visible (define winning before testing); Focus the team (one problem at a time); Prove impact (signals tie back to intent, not vanity metrics).
- Bad habit to break: jumping to solutions without clarity. Better habit: always define a concept first so signals have meaning.
- **Step 1 — Concepts → Defining Intent**. Inputs: business problems, current solutions, organizational priorities. Outputs: a clear concept framed by user needs and business goals.
  - Start with the business problem or friction point.
  - Bring forward user needs from *Define* (with their UX metrics).
  - Map against business goals from *Lead* (with their business metrics).
  - Capture loose requirements — boundaries, not specs.
  - Narrow to one actionable concept at a time.
  - Example: User Need (Define) = Clarity in checkout (comprehension + error rate); Business Goal (Lead) = Reduce payment drop-offs (conversion rate); Concept (Measure) = Redesign payment step to reduce abandonment.
  - 5-minute test: if you can't write user need + business goal + signal in under 5 min, the concept isn't clear.
- **List of concepts** — 200+ Concept pages organized by category: Products (Dashboard Engagement, Onboarding Walkthroughs, Account Management); Websites (Landing Page for Ads, Checkout Flows, Product Detail Pages); Mobile Apps (Login Flows, Push Notifications, AR Features); E-commerce (Shopping Cart, Wishlist, Order Tracking); Marketing (Campaign Microsites, Case Studies, Social Media Creatives).
- **Step 2 — Ideation → Hunches & Questions**. Hunches give a starting point as testable hypotheses tied to UX metrics; Questions sharpen them into measurable signals. Hunch template: *"We believe that [change] for [audience] will [impact] because [reason]."* Question crafting: clarify initiative, identify gaps, turn gaps into goals, write open-ended research questions, translate to testable, align with UX metrics, prioritize.
- **Step 3 — Findings → Turning Data into Signals**. Inputs: measurement data. Outputs: signals tied to business and customer outcomes. A signal forms when metrics tie to a design choice, a user need, and a business goal. Process: translate data → findings → tie to user value → tie to business results → connect back to intent → act → share widely.
- **From Intent to Signals (summary loop)**: (1) Concepts define intent, (2) Ideation produces hunches & questions, (3) Findings turn data into signals — shrinking big initiatives into focused efforts and replacing debate with evidence.

## Glare | Measure, Hunches v1.0

Hunches are informed guesses — testable bets that turn instinct, early signs, and experience into measurable hypotheses.

- **What a hunch is**: An informed guess; a shaped instinct about what might be true. *"We believe there is something important here, and we are ready to test it."*
- A strong hunch is: tied to a user problem/friction point; expressed as a clear, provable hypothesis; linked to a metric so results are visible; **falsifiable** — could be wrong, and that's the point.
- **Ideation context**: Ideation is the widest, messiest creative space. Hunches narrow it ("we believe…"); Questions sharpen ("how can we prove this?"); the overlap is where evidence forms.
- **STEP 1 — Start with Context**: Tie the hunch to a business goal, design principle, or user-journey moment. Ask what you're designing, who for, and what you already know. Make hunch-building shared (standups, sketch sessions, research reviews). First hunch won't be the last.
- **STEP 2 — Capture Initial Assumptions**. Spark techniques: Survey Data, Ideation Sketching, Empathy Mapping, Customer Journey Framework, Stakeholder Interviews, Customer Feedback, Data Analytics, Competitive Analysis, Expert Audit.
- **STEP 3 — Spot Opportunities**: Focus on the problem, not the solution. Ask what feels clunky or confusing, where unmet needs exist, where you can add value or reduce effort.
- **STEP 4 — Form Your Hunch**. Belief statement: *"We believe users need ___ so they can ___."* Then design hypothesis: *"We believe that [this change] for [this group] will [have this impact] because [supporting reason]."*
  - Weak: "We believe users like shorter forms."
  - Stronger: "We believe removing two steps from the signup form for first-time users will increase completion rates because users drop off at step 3."
- **STEP 5 — Translate Into Questions**: What behavior would confirm/disprove the hunch? What needs to happen for it to be true? How will we know? Link to a UX metric (completion rate, task time, drop-off). Treat results as signals, not final answers.
- **Benefits of using hunches**: helps focus; avoids building the wrong thing; gets team aligned early; reduces documentation overhead; builds better features grounded in early user signals; keeps a learning loop alive post-launch.
- **How Glare helps**: Glare turns hunches into focused, testable questions so teams move fast without waiting for full research, bridging creative thinking and user-centered decisions.

## Glare | Measure, Questioning v1.0

The first version of the Questioning module: a stepped process for creating research questions that produce useful UX measurement.

- **Why questioning matters**: Weak questions stall in indecision; strong questions expose where users struggle, what they value, and whether the design works. Questioning aligns metrics to intent.
- **What good questioning does**: surfaces hidden challenges; avoids bias; guides design with targeted insights; translates curiosity into measurable data.
- **STEP 1 — Clarify the Initiative**. Ask: what are we trying to learn/improve/validate? What decision will this inform? Where in the process are we (exploration, prototyping, post-launch)? Who needs the insight? What's the risk if we don't get it right?
- **Types of Questions** (4 categories): **People** (habits/preferences/behaviors — "How often do users complete this workflow?"); **Process** (steps + friction — "What steps do users take to upload a file?"); **Product** (clarity/usefulness — "Do users understand the purpose of this page?"); **Problem** (barriers — "What prevents users from completing checkout?").
- **STEP 2 — Identify Knowledge Gaps**: List unknowns important to the decision. Check user mindset/context — language they'll understand, what matters in the moment — to avoid jargon.
- **STEP 3 — Turn Gaps into Broad Learning Goals**: Define what to learn before drafting questions. Distinguish strategic (broad, long-term) vs. tactical (specific feature/flow).
- **STEP 4 — Write Open-Ended Research Questions**: Open-ended, user-focused, unbiased. Bias check: is it leading? Echoing internal assumptions? Emotionally loaded or too technical?
- **STEP 5 — Translate to Testable Questions**: Frame so each connects to a technique and yields actionable feedback. Examples: "Can users locate pricing within 5 seconds?" → First Click Testing; "Do users understand the page purpose?" → Survey + Heatmap; "What stops checkout?" → Clickstream + Task Success.
- **UX Metric Types table**: Behavioral (what users do — heatmaps, clickstream); Attitudinal (what they think/feel — surveys); Performance (how well they complete tasks — task success, time on task).
- **Testable question criteria**: Specific & focused; linked to measurable outcome; actionable; aligned with user goals (not just business). Weak: "Do people like our homepage?" Strong: "Can users find pricing info on the homepage within 5 seconds?"
- **STEP 6 — Align Questions with UX Metrics**: Mapping table — task success ↔ completion rate; time on task ↔ efficiency; survey + comprehension ↔ clarity; first click ↔ intuition/desirability.
- **STEP 7 — Prioritize and Refine**: by relevance to design decision, feasibility (timeline, resources), and impact on outcome.
- **STEP 8 — Match Questions to Techniques**: Observe action → behavioral techniques (Clickstream, Heatmaps); Measure performance → task-based metrics (Completion, Time); Understand opinions → attitudinal methods (Surveys).
- The Glare Questioning Canvas exists as a Figma-style React layout with form-style input boxes (light theme) for running this process.

## Glare | Measure, Questioning v1.1

Evolved version of Questioning. Adds a **Mode of Questioning** layer (Exploratory/Evaluative/Comparative), reframes the 8-step process around the Concepts→Hunches→Questions→Findings rhythm, and adds workflow-integration guidance.

- **Section structure**: Getting Started → Begin Questioning → Make It Practical → See It in Action → Apply and Evolve.
- **Element-to-Questioning mapping**: Concepts (initial ideas → questioning defines how they're evaluated); Hunches (early beliefs → questioning exposes whether they hold); Questions (focused prompts → questioning structures investigation); Findings (emerging patterns → questioning supplies the input).
- **4 Question Types** (same as v1.0): People, Process, Product, Problem. People + Process give context; Product + Problem give clarity.
- **What makes a question testable** (3 criteria): defines a gap in understanding; points to observable behavior or perception; can be measured and acted upon. ❌ "Do people like our homepage?" / ✅ "Can users find pricing info on the homepage within 5 seconds?" Pre-test by having teammates review and running a small dry test.
- **Modes of Questioning**:
  - **Exploratory** — early discovery, no firm hypotheses; "What should we learn or improve?"; yields patterns, context, unmet needs.
  - **Evaluative** — mid-cycle, designs exist; "Does this design work for users?"; yields clarity, comprehension, usability metrics.
  - **Comparative** — late-stage, choosing between directions; "Which version performs better?"; yields confidence and proof of improvement.
- **8-Step Process (v1.1 phrasing)**:
  1. **Clarify the concept** — surface assumptions; what success looks like if hunch is right.
  2. **Identify knowledge gaps** — what's untested, where confidence is weak, what would surprise you if false.
  3. **Define learning goals** — pick mode (Exploratory/Evaluative/Comparative) and frame the goal.
  4. **Write open-ended research questions** — explore perception/experience without assumption; bias check.
  5. **Translate into testable questions** — observable behavior/perception, point directly to data.
  6. **Align with UX metrics** — Behavioral (First Click Success), Attitudinal (Satisfaction/Sentiment), Performance (Time on Task), Perceptual (Comprehension).
  7. **Prioritize and refine** — filters: Gap Size, Impact, Feasibility, Confidence; questions at intersection of large-gap + high-impact + feasible rise to top.
  8. **Match questions to techniques** — Behavioral (first-click, task success), Attitudinal (surveys, desirability, satisfaction scoring), Performance (usability tests, time tracking, error rate).
- **Make It Practical — Worksheet** mirrors the 8 steps with action + output for each, becoming a roadmap for a questioning cycle. Pre-cycle checklist confirms one concept, identified gaps, clear learning mode, metric per question, prioritization, ready techniques, and connection to findings.
- **Integrate into Workflow** (repeatable routine):
  1. **Weekly Discovery Review** — identify assumptions/behaviors needing clarity.
  2. **Sprint Planning** — pair every major task with at least one measurable question on the board.
  3. **Post-Test Reflection** — review which questions produced usable signals; refine biased/broad ones.
  4. **Share the questions, not just the answers** — include the question that led to a finding.
  5. **Build a Question Library** — collect well-framed, tested questions for reuse.
- **Business impact**: Faster Focus (cuts discovery cycles to hours); Better Decisions (credible findings); Cross-Team Alignment (shared frameworks across product/design/marketing); Proven Value (measurable insight → conversion/churn impact).
- **Reflection prompts**: Did questions address biggest gaps? Which produced strong findings vs. stayed broad? Was each tied to a UX metric? How did answers change the next decision? What will we ask differently?
- **Next-step transitions table**: Concepts→Hunches (clarify belief & learning need) → Hunches→Questioning (turn beliefs into measurable prompts) → Questioning→Findings (collect & analyze through metrics) → Findings→Signals (combine metrics into patterns proving impact).

## Glare | Measure, Findings v1.0

Findings are the final move of Measure: they turn raw data into design signals tied to both user value and business outcomes.

- **Why findings matter**: Data alone (drop-offs, survey scores) only tells what happened. Findings make data evidence — connecting it to user needs and business goals so every metric ties back to intent.
- **What a finding is**: An observation turned into direction; takes patterns from research and connects them to a design choice. A strong finding is: tied to a design decision; linked to a user need; connected to a business goal; expressed as a clear, actionable design signal.
- **Step 1 — Translate Data Into Findings**. Pair the metric with its source and describe the behavior. Ask: what are users doing/struggling with, and how do we know? Example — Data: 48% drop-off at payment step (checkout analytics). Finding: Analytics show nearly half of users abandon checkout when choosing a payment option.
- **Step 2 — Tie to User Value**. Map finding to a specific user need (comprehension, usefulness, satisfaction, clarity, trust). Ask: which user need does this reveal or threaten? Example — User Value: Clarity, users need payment choices to be simple and error-free.
- **Step 3 — Tie to Business Results**. Link to a business metric leadership cares about (conversion, retention, efficiency, revenue). Ask: if we fix this, how does it move the business? Example — Reducing abandonment increases conversion and revenue.
- **Step 4 — Connect Back to Intent**. Cross-check the finding against the original concept/hunch. If it doesn't answer it, it's noise. Example — Hunch: "Simplifying payment will reduce abandonment." Finding confirms hunch and validates concept.
- **Step 5 — Act on Signals**. Write recommendation tied to both user and business metrics. Worked example:
  - Data: 48% drop-off at payment (checkout analytics)
  - Finding: Confusing payment options cause abandonment
  - User Need: Clarity — choose payment without errors
  - Business Goal: Reduce abandonment → increase conversion
  - **Signal**: Simplifying payment options will increase completion (business metric) and lower error rate (user metric).
- **Why this matters**: UX metrics tell you what happened; signals tell you what to do next. Without findings, teams stall in debates; with them, ideas earn their way forward fast. Findings close the loop — research → signals → action.

## Key terms

- **Glare** — Helio's overall design framework. Has multiple facets; Measure is the second.
- **Fog** — The default state of design indecision: stalled debates, late proof, lost momentum. Glare/Measure exists to "cut through" it.
- **Measure** — The Glare facet for testing usefulness and turning intuition into evidence-backed signals via Concepts → Hunches → Questioning → Findings.
- **Define** — Adjacent Glare facet that supplies user needs and UX metrics into Measure.
- **Lead** — Adjacent Glare facet that supplies business goals and business metrics into Measure.
- **Concept** — A focused, testable design effort framed by user need + business goal + signal to measure. The intent layer. Over 200 Concept pages exist across Products / Websites / Mobile Apps / E-commerce / Marketing.
- **Hunch** — An informed, falsifiable guess expressed as a hypothesis. Template: *"We believe that [change] for [audience] will [impact] because [reason]."*
- **Hypothesis** — A structured, testable form of a hunch tied to a measurable outcome.
- **Question (Research vs. Testable)** — Research questions are open-ended and user-focused; testable questions point to observable behavior/perception and a measurable outcome.
- **Question Types** — People, Process, Product, Problem. People+Process = context; Product+Problem = clarity.
- **Modes of Questioning** — Exploratory (discover), Evaluative (validate clarity/usability), Comparative (decide between options). v1.1 addition.
- **UX Metric Types** — Behavioral (what users do), Attitudinal (what they think/feel), Performance (how efficiently they succeed), Perceptual (what they understand) — last added in v1.1.
- **Finding** — An observation translated into direction, linked to a user need, business goal, and design decision.
- **Signal** — Evidence-backed recommendation that tells the team what to do next; the output of the Measure loop. Replaces opinion-based debate.
- **Vanity metrics** — Metrics disconnected from intent; what concepts/signals are designed to avoid.
- **Falsifiable** — A hunch/hypothesis property: it could be proven wrong, which is the point.
- **Bias check** — Pre-flight on questions: is it leading? echoing assumptions? emotionally loaded? too technical?
- **Question Library** — Reusable, well-framed, tested questions stored in a shared workspace for cross-project consistency.
- **Glare Questioning Canvas** — Figma-style React layout (light theme, form input boxes) that operationalizes the questioning steps.

## Failure modes

These are common ways this skill goes wrong in practice:

- **Skipping Concepts.** Jumping into Hunches and Questions without anchoring intent first. The Measure work then produces signals that don't tie back to anything Focus or Lead can use.
- **Hunches as ideation.** Treating hunch-writing as brainstorm sessions without falsifiability. Hunches must be bets that could be wrong; without that, they shape nothing.
- **Findings without translation.** Reporting raw data as the deliverable instead of running it through the user-need + business-result chain. Data alone is noise — the Findings move turns it into signal.
- **Treating the four moves as a one-time pipeline.** Concepts → Hunches → Questions → Findings is a *loop* — findings produce new concepts. Linear teams stop after one pass; loop teams compound learning.
