# How Glare Works — Compressed (v1.3)

## Why Glare exists at this altitude

Most teams aren't short on input. There are always ideas on the table, feedback coming in, different perspectives shaping the work. Prompts generate directions. What slows things down isn't lack of ideas — **it's what happens when the team needs to decide what to do next**.

Glare gives structure to that moment. It connects how ideas are explored, how they're evaluated, and how decisions are carried forward so the work doesn't lose direction under pressure. Built around four connected parts:

- Design Signals
- Decision Map
- Design Review
- Design Assessment

---

## 1. Design Signals — the unit of clarity

A design signal turns a vague sense of "this feels right" or "this might be off" into something the team can actually work with.

A signal connects:
- a **decision** the team needs to make
- a **measurable user response**
- the **outcome** that decision is meant to influence

This is where the work starts to become clear. Instead of reacting to opinions or scattered feedback, the team has something concrete to respond to. You can see how users are interpreting the experience, where things break, and how different directions compare.

**AI Skills work underneath**, shaping how signals are created. They help turn rough ideas into testable concepts and define what should be measured so signals are consistent, not one-off.

When signals are strong, conversations change. The team is no longer guessing — they're responding to something real.

---

## 2. Decision Map — how decisions take shape

Once signals exist: what do we do with them?

The Decision Map shows how decisions take shape and move forward through the work. **Not as a rigid process** — as a way to understand where you are and what needs to happen next. Teams move back and forth across these areas. It's not linear. But the structure makes it easier to see where a decision is getting stuck.

| Area | What it asks |
|---|---|
| **Define** | What problem are we actually solving? What matters here? |
| **Measure** | How is this performing? What are users showing us? |
| **Focus** | Which direction is stronger? What should move forward? |
| **Lead** | What do we commit to, and how does it connect to outcomes? |

AI Skills support this flow by keeping evaluation consistent. They ensure signals connect to decisions the same way each time, instead of being interpreted differently depending on the conversation.

---

## 3. Design Review — where signals are applied

This is where most teams feel the friction.

Design Review is where work is presented, feedback is shared, and decisions are expected to happen. It's also where things tend to slow down. Without structure, reviews expand. More feedback comes in. New ideas are introduced. The conversation keeps going, but the decision doesn't land.

**Signals change that dynamic.** They give the team something to anchor on. Instead of reacting to everything, the conversation stays focused on what matters and what the signal is actually showing.

In a strong review:
- attention stays on the decision
- tradeoffs are visible
- feedback connects back to signals
- the team can commit to a direction

AI Skills help keep that structure in place — they guide how work is evaluated so reviews don't drift. When this works, **reviews stop being a place where work gets discussed and start becoming a place where work moves forward.**

---

## 4. Design Assessment — how the system holds over time

Even when teams make good decisions, those decisions don't always carry. They get revisited. They lose clarity as they move across teams. Or they never fully connect to outcomes.

Design Assessment looks at that layer. It focuses on how decisions move through the system over time, **not just whether a single moment went well**.

It helps answer:
- are signals being created consistently?
- are decisions actually landing?
- is the work holding as it moves forward?

To do that, Glare looks at the work across **five dimensions**. These reflect where decisions tend to break:

| Dimension | What it diagnoses |
|---|---|
| **Organizing Work** | Is the work structured clearly, or does it feel scattered and reactive? |
| **Managing Complexity** | Are teams able to handle multiple directions and inputs, or does complexity slow decisions? |
| **Building Proof** | Are signals strong and consistent, or does the work rely on assumptions and opinion? |
| **Guiding Decisions** | Are signals actually used to make decisions, or do conversations drift without resolution? |
| **Scaling Influence** | Do decisions carry across teams and over time, or do they get revisited and lose momentum? |

This is less about a single moment and more about patterns. Over time, these dimensions show where the system is working and where it's breaking.

AI Skills support this by making evaluation repeatable. They standardize how signals are created, how decisions are assessed, and how gaps are identified. Without that consistency, it's easy to assume things are working until friction builds up. **Design Assessment makes that visible early**, so teams can strengthen the system before decisions start to stall.

---

## How it comes together (the loop)

When all of this connects, the work feels different. Instead of bouncing between ideas and feedback, there's a clear path from exploration to decision.

```
prompts/conversations  →  ideas
                ↓
         AI Skills  →  shape how those ideas are evaluated
                ↓
          signals  →  show how users respond
                ↓
       Design Review  →  uses signals to guide decisions
                ↓
       Decision Map  →  keeps decisions moving (Define/Measure/Focus/Lead)
                ↓
   Design Assessment  →  shows how well it's all holding together
```

Each step feeds the next. The output of one becomes the input of another. **The loop repeats, but it doesn't reset. It builds.**

---

## What this produces

When Glare is working well, the outputs are noticeable. The team produces:

- design signals that clearly reflect user behavior
- decisions that people can align around
- visible tradeoffs between options
- direction that holds as work moves forward
- a clear connection between design work and outcomes

More importantly, **the team spends less time circling decisions and more time moving them forward**.

---

## Strong vs. weak Design Review (worked example)

**Weak review** — work is presented, every stakeholder reacts from their own angle, feedback piles up, new ideas are introduced, no one feels confident enough to call direction. Decision punted to next meeting.

**Strong review** — signal is on the table. Conversation focuses on what the signal is showing across the directions being considered. Tradeoffs are visible because the metric makes them visible. Feedback that doesn't connect to the signal is parked. Decision lands in the room.

---

## Strong vs. weak signal flow at the org level (Design Assessment lens)

**Strong flow** — signals are created consistently across teams, decisions land in reviews, those decisions carry into the next initiative without being relitigated, leaders can see how design choices connect to outcomes.

**Weak flow** — signals exist on individual teams but don't travel; decisions get made then revisited; proof stays trapped in the design team; leaders see effort but not impact. The Design Assessment surfaces *where* in the five dimensions the break is happening so leaders can act.
