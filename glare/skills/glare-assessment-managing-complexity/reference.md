# Managing Complexity — Design Assessment Dimension

Compressed from "Glare | Design Assessment, Managing Complexity v1.3" (May 3, 2026).

> "Design work gets harder when teams cannot hold the system together."

## What this dimension measures

How well your team handles work that **spans products, teams, channels, tools, constraints, and decisions**. Strong design teams do not simplify by ignoring complexity. They create enough structure to move through it. The dimension shows whether complexity is helping the team make better decisions or slowing the work down.

A team strong in this dimension can clearly answer:

- What level of complexity are we dealing with?
- What parts of the work are simple and repeatable?
- What parts require coordination and expertise?
- What parts are uncertain and need learning?
- Where are the dependencies?
- Who needs to stay aligned?
- What structure will help the work move?

## When to use

- When the user is diagnosing why work keeps tangling, dependencies surprise the team, or every project feels overweight.
- When the team can't separate Simple (just do it) from Complicated (coordination) from Complex (learn-as-you-go) work.
- When plans get locked before the problem is understood — or when simple repeatable tasks get crushed under heavy process.
- When work is growing across products, teams, and decisions and the existing structure isn't scaling.
- When ownership inside complicated work is unclear and surprises land late.

**Don't use when** the user wants the broader assessment (`glare-design-assessment`), is in a different dimension, or is interpreting the overall score pattern.

## Why it matters

Most product and design work does not stay simple for long. A small idea touches more screens. A flow connects to more systems. A decision affects more teams. The problem is not that the work is complex — it is that teams often do not have a shared way to manage it.

When that happens:

- teams lose sight of what matters most
- dependencies slow progress
- decisions get pushed across teams
- handoffs create confusion
- edge cases take over the conversation
- the original user need gets buried
- leaders see activity but not clear movement

## Where momentum breaks

Momentum breaks when the team treats all work the same. Some work needs a checklist. Some needs coordination. Some needs discovery. When teams use the same process for every kind of problem, the work either gets over-managed or under-managed.

You see weak Managing Complexity when:

- simple work gets slowed down by too much process
- complicated work lacks clear owners or handoffs
- complex work gets forced into fixed plans too early
- teams disagree on what kind of problem they are solving
- dependencies are discovered too late
- decisions stall because too many pieces are connected
- the user need gets lost inside operational constraints

The issue is not that the team cannot handle complexity. It is that the complexity is not being **named, structured, or managed** clearly.

## What strong looks like

Strong Managing Complexity makes larger work easier to move. The team can tell the difference between simple, complicated, and complex work. It knows when to rely on known patterns, when to coordinate expertise, and when to create space for learning.

Strong teams:

- name the type of complexity early
- separate simple tasks from uncertain problems
- clarify dependencies before they block progress
- define ownership across teams
- keep the user need visible as the work grows
- use the right level of structure for the problem
- adjust the plan as new signals appear
- help stakeholders understand tradeoffs

This does not mean removing complexity. It means making complexity easier to work with.

## What weak looks like

Weak Managing Complexity creates confusion and drag. Every decision creates another dependency. Every dependency creates another meeting. Progress depends on individual effort instead of a shared way to move through the system.

Weak teams often:

- treat every problem as if it has the same level of uncertainty
- over-process simple work
- under-structure complicated work
- lock complex work too early
- miss dependencies until they become blockers
- struggle to align product, design, engineering, and leadership
- lose the user problem inside internal constraints
- revisit decisions because the full system was not understood

This makes design feel slow, even when the team is moving fast.

## How it is evaluated — the three levels

Managing Complexity uses a different lens than Organizing Work. Where Organizing Work uses the Decision Map to track how knowledge moves, Managing Complexity looks at the **type of problem** and whether the team is using the right approach for that level. The assessment focuses on three levels:

```
Simple → Complicated → Complex
```

These show whether the team can recognize what kind of work it is doing and apply the right amount of structure, coordination, and learning.

### Level 1 — Simple

Simple work is **stable, repeatable, and already understood**. Familiar patterns, known workflows, common interface changes, routine updates, tasks where the team already knows what good looks like.

- **Strong:** the team has clear standards and moves quickly without overthinking every step.
- **Weak:** small tasks become heavier than they need to be. Teams add meetings, reviews, and custom decisions to work that should be handled through shared patterns.

> Simple work needs **clarity, not complexity.**

### Level 2 — Complicated

Complicated work has **many parts but can be managed with expertise and coordination**. Multi-step flows, cross-team handoffs, system dependencies, technical constraints, compliance needs, larger initiatives where the path is knowable but requires alignment.

- **Strong:** the team identifies dependencies early, brings in the right expertise, and creates enough structure to keep teams moving together.
- **Weak:** ownership gets blurry. Teams miss handoffs, discover constraints too late, or keep reopening decisions because the system was not fully considered.

> Complicated work needs **coordination, not guesswork.**

### Level 3 — Complex

Complex work is **uncertain, adaptive, and hard to predict upfront**. New product directions, unclear user behavior, emerging markets, AI-enabled workflows, major strategy shifts, problems where the team does not yet know what will work.

- **Strong:** the team creates space to learn. It uses signals to reduce uncertainty, tests assumptions early, and adapts as new patterns appear.
- **Weak:** the team pretends it has certainty too soon. Plans get locked before the problem is understood. Teams push toward delivery without enough evidence, then struggle when reality does not match the plan.

> Complex work needs **learning, not premature certainty.**

## How to strengthen Managing Complexity

Start by naming the kind of complexity you are dealing with. You do not need a new process for every project. You need a clearer way to decide what kind of work it is and what level of structure it needs.

The goal is to create a clearer path:

```
Simple → Complicated → Complex
```

That path should show what can move quickly, what needs coordination, and what needs more learning before the team commits.

**Useful moves:**

- separate repeatable tasks from uncertain problems
- name the dependencies early
- identify which teams need to stay aligned
- clarify where expertise is needed
- define what decisions can be made now
- define what still needs a signal
- keep the user need visible as constraints grow
- use lighter process for simple work
- use stronger coordination for complicated work
- use testing and discovery for complex work

The goal is not to reduce everything to a simple plan. It is to manage complexity without losing momentum.

## Diagnostic questions

Use these to evaluate how well your team manages complexity:

- Are we clear on what kind of problem this is?
- What parts of the work are simple and repeatable?
- What parts require coordination across teams?
- What parts are still uncertain?
- What dependencies could slow us down?
- Who needs to be involved before decisions are made?
- Are we forcing certainty too early?
- What signal would help us move forward with more confidence?

## What improves when this is strong

When Managing Complexity is strong, teams move through bigger work with less confusion:

- simple work moves faster
- complicated work gets clearer ownership
- complex work gets the learning it needs before teams overcommit
- unnecessary process reduces
- dependencies get clarified sooner
- teams align around the right level of effort
- premature decisions are avoided
- user needs stay visible through constraints
- tradeoffs become easier to explain
- large initiatives move forward with less drag

This is where design maturity becomes easier to sustain as the work grows.

## Failure modes

These are common ways this skill goes wrong in practice:

- **Treating complex work as complicated.** Locking a plan, naming milestones, and tracking burndown for work that is actually still being figured out. Symptoms: replans every sprint, "we hit the milestone but the work is wrong," scope creep that's actually scope discovery.
- **Treating simple work as complex.** Wrapping a small, repeatable change in research rounds, multiple reviews, and discovery — turning a 2-day fix into a 4-week project. The structure becomes the work.
- **Hiding complexity by splitting it.** Breaking complex work into many small "complicated" tickets so it fits the existing system. Each ticket finishes, but the whole still doesn't add up because the underlying learning never happened.
- **No shared vocabulary for the three levels.** Without Simple / Complicated / Complex as named modes, every project feels the same and the team can't ask "what kind of problem is this?" before choosing a structure.

## How this dimension supports the others

When complexity is easier to name and structure, teams can:

- **organize work** with clearer context
- **build proof** from better-scoped problems
- **guide decisions** with fewer hidden dependencies
- **scale influence** because leaders can see how the work fits together

## Smallest-leverage next move

Pick one current initiative. Name the level of complexity (Simple, Complicated, or Complex). Look for the **mismatch**: is the team over-processing simple work, under-coordinating complicated work, or pretending complex work is more certain than it is? That single naming move usually reveals the next improvement.

Do **not** start by adopting a new project management framework. Start with one initiative, named correctly.

## Note on terminology

This dimension uses Simple / Complicated / Complex — it does not use the Decision Map's Define / Measure / Focus / Lead axis. (The Decision Map's "Show" was renamed to "Lead" in v1.3, but Managing Complexity does not use that naming.) The Decision Map informs how knowledge moves through the work; Managing Complexity informs how structure should match the work.
