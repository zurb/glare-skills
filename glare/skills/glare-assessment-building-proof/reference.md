# Building Proof — Design Assessment Dimension

Compressed from "Glare | Design Assessment, Building Proof v1.3" (May 3, 2026).

> "Design work gets weaker when teams cannot show what changed."

## What this dimension measures

How well your team **connects design work to user outcomes and business results**. Strong design teams do not rely on belief alone. They show how the work improved the experience and why that improvement matters. The dimension shows whether design impact becomes visible or stays trapped inside the team.

A team strong in this dimension can clearly answer:

- What problem were we trying to solve?
- What did we believe would improve?
- What signal showed whether it worked?
- What changed for users?
- What changed for the business?
- What evidence supports the story?
- How can we show the impact clearly?

## Why it matters

Most teams believe their design work matters. They improve flows, simplify screens, run research, shape concepts, reduce friction. But when the work reaches leadership, the proof is often hard to see.

When that happens:

- design impact stays abstract
- teams struggle to explain what changed
- results are described as activity instead of outcomes
- leaders do not see the connection to business goals
- good work gets treated like opinion
- future investment becomes harder to defend
- design loses influence even when the work improves the experience

The work may be strong, but the proof does not travel.

## Where momentum breaks

Momentum breaks when design work cannot be connected to results. A team improves an experience, but if no one can show what changed, the impact gets watered down. A test reveals useful signals, but they're not tied to outcomes. A project supports a business goal, but the connection is unclear.

You see weak Building Proof when:

- design wins are described without numbers or signals
- research findings do not connect to business goals
- teams show deliverables instead of outcomes
- leaders ask, "What did this change?"
- UX metrics are collected but not used in the proof story
- before and after comparisons are missing
- design impact depends on who is presenting it
- teams struggle to justify continued investment

The issue is not that design lacks impact. It is that the impact has not been **made visible**.

## What strong looks like

Strong Building Proof makes design impact easier to see. The team connects the purpose of the work to signals, outcomes, and results. It can show what changed, why it mattered, and how the work supported the larger goal.

Strong teams:

- define what proof would look like before the work starts
- connect design intent to user outcomes
- choose UX metrics that show meaningful change
- compare before and after performance
- connect findings to decisions
- show how decisions affected results
- explain design impact in language leaders understand
- make proof reusable across projects and teams

This does not mean every project needs a large measurement plan. It means the team knows what kind of proof would make the work easier to trust.

## What weak looks like

Weak Building Proof makes design value harder to see. The team may produce strong work, but the proof is scattered, delayed, or missing. The story depends on visuals, explanations, or opinions instead of evidence.

Weak teams often:

- start work without defining what success means
- collect research without tying it to outcomes
- measure activity instead of change
- show screens without explaining impact
- use metrics that do not connect to user needs
- struggle to compare versions or progress over time
- rely on anecdotes when leaders need proof
- lose the impact story after the project ends

This makes design feel less strategic, even when the work creates value.

## How it is evaluated — the four layers

Building Proof looks at how proof forms over time, from early discovery to business results. The assessment focuses on four layers that form a chain:

```
Research → Intent → Outcome → Impact
```

These show whether the team can move from raw research to clear intent, then from outcomes to visible impact.

### Layer 1 — Research

How well the team **gathers evidence** about the problem, user need, or opportunity. Includes exploratory studies, user feedback, interviews, survey responses, behavioral signals, early patterns. Research helps the team understand what matters before deciding what to change.

- **Strong:** the team can explain what users need and why the work matters.
- **Weak:** the team starts from assumptions, stakeholder opinions, or unclear problem framing.

> Research gives proof its **starting point.**

### Layer 2 — Intent

How well the team **turns research into a clear direction**. Includes hypotheses, goals, success definitions, hunches, and design choices tied to a specific outcome. Intent explains what the team believes the work should improve.

- **Strong:** the team can explain what it expected to change and why.
- **Weak:** design decisions feel disconnected from evidence. The team may make improvements but cannot say what those improvements were meant to prove.

> Intent gives proof a **target.**

### Layer 3 — Outcome

Whether the design change **worked for users**. Includes UX metric results — task success, comprehension, satisfaction, findability, error rate, time on task, preference, adoption — and before-and-after comparisons.

- **Strong:** the team can show what changed in the experience.
- **Weak:** the team has feedback or activity, but not enough evidence the design improved anything meaningful.

> Outcome gives proof its **evidence.**

### Layer 4 — Impact

How design outcomes **connect to business results**. Includes growth, conversion, retention, efficiency, risk reduction, support reduction, adoption, confidence, strategic movement. Impact explains why the design outcome matters beyond the design team.

- **Strong:** the team can show how improved user outcomes support goals leaders care about.
- **Weak:** design proof stays local. The team may show that something tested well, but not why that improvement matters to the business.

> Impact gives proof its **influence.**

## How to strengthen Building Proof

Start by defining what proof would look like **before** the work begins. You do not need a complex analytics model or a large research program. Start with one project and create a clearer trail from research to impact.

The goal is to create a clearer proof chain:

```
Research → Intent → Outcome → Impact
```

That chain should show what the team learned, what it aimed to improve, what changed for users, and why that change mattered.

**Useful moves:**

- define the user problem clearly
- connect the problem to a business goal
- write down what the team expects to improve
- choose one or two UX metrics that fit the work
- capture a baseline before changing the design
- compare versions or track before-and-after movement
- summarize what changed in plain language
- connect the result to a decision
- explain why the result matters to leadership

The goal is not proof for its own sake. It is to make design impact easier to see, trust, and act on.

## Diagnostic questions

Use these to evaluate how well your team builds proof:

- What user need or problem started the work?
- What did we expect the design to improve?
- What metric or signal showed whether it worked?
- Did we capture a baseline or comparison?
- What changed for users?
- What changed for the business?
- Can leaders understand why this result matters?
- Can this proof support the next decision?

## What improves when this is strong

When Building Proof is strong, design becomes easier to defend and scale:

- teams spend less time explaining why design matters
- leaders see how design choices affect users and support business goals
- future work gets stronger because each project adds evidence
- design work connects to outcomes
- user value becomes easier to measure
- decisions can be explained with evidence
- progress over time becomes visible
- trust with leadership grows
- continued investment becomes easier to justify
- design wins turn into reusable proof stories

This is where design maturity becomes visible.

## How this dimension supports the others

When proof is easier to see, teams can:

- **organize work** around stronger evidence
- **manage complexity** with clearer outcomes
- **guide decisions** with more confidence
- **scale influence** because leaders can see the value

## Smallest-leverage next move

Pick one recent project. Trace the proof chain: Research → Intent → Outcome → Impact. Find the weakest link.

Even better: pick one project that is *just starting* and write down — before the work begins — what proof would make it easier to trust. That single act prevents most weak-proof failures.

Do **not** start by buying analytics tooling or rolling out a measurement framework. Start with one project, one cleaner chain.

## Note on terminology

Building Proof's four layers (Research, Intent, Outcome, Impact) are not the same as the Decision Map's Define / Measure / Focus / Lead phases. They form a chain about evidence, not an operating cycle. (The Decision Map's "Show" phase was renamed to "Lead" in v1.3.) The two complement each other — Building Proof tells you whether the proof chain is intact; the Decision Map tells you where in the work each block sits.
