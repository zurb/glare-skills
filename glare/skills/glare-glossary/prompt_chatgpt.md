## Core Framework / Methodology

### Core terms (alphabetical)

**AI Agent** — An AI system that can manage a goal, choose steps, and use Skills to complete work. In Glare, **Ray** (ZURB's agent) helps coordinate parts of the workflow — applying a Skill, structuring input, comparing signals.

**AI Skill** — A reusable way of working that gives AI and teams a consistent structure to complete a task. *A prompt tells AI what to do in one moment. A Skill defines how to do something repeatedly.* Skills help teams frame decisions, clarify user needs, connect business goals, shape signals, interpret results.

**AI Workflow** — The way prompts, Skills, agents, signals, and teams work together to move from idea to decision. A simple AI workflow: prompt generates an idea -> Skill structures the work -> agent coordinates the steps -> participant input creates a signal -> team makes the decision.

**Business Goal** — The outcome the team or organization wants to move. Connects design work to what the company cares about — conversion, activation, retention, trust, efficiency, growth.

**Decision** — A clear choice about what moves forward. Might mean choosing one direction, refining an idea, stopping something that's not working, changing scope, or creating another signal. In Glare, decisions are supported by signals.

**Decision Map** — The part of Glare that shows how design signals are strengthened through the work. Organizes decision activity into four areas: **Define / Measure / Focus / Lead**. Helps teams understand where a decision is, what information is needed, and what should happen next.

**Define** — The Decision Map area focused on **clarifying what matters**. User need, audience, context, what success should mean.

**Design Assessment** — A way to see how well design decisions move through an organization. Looks across **five dimensions**: Organizing Work, Managing Complexity, Building Proof, Guiding Decisions, Scaling Influence. Helps teams see where signals are strong, where decisions break down, where the system needs support.

**Design Impact** — The change created by a design decision. Becomes clearer when a decision connects to a user need, a UX metric, a design signal, and a product or business outcome.

**Design Review** — The moment where work is discussed, evaluated, and expected to move toward a decision. In Glare, Design Review is **where signals are applied** so conversations stay focused and decisions can land.

**Design Signal** — The smallest unit of clarity a team can use to move a decision forward. A design signal connects: a UX metric + intuition + direction + context + user need + business goal. Signals help teams understand what users are showing and how that should shape a decision.

**Focus** — The Decision Map area focused on **comparing options and choosing direction**. Uses signals to decide what should move forward.

**Hunch** — An early read on the work, **before evidence is clear**. Sounds like "users might get stuck here," "this direction feels stronger," "this could improve conversion." Becomes useful when shaped into a signal.

**Intuition** — What the team is noticing based on experience, judgment, and pattern recognition. **Glare does not remove intuition.** It gives intuition structure so teams can connect it to user needs, metrics, and decisions.

**Lead** — The Decision Map area focused on **connecting decisions to outcomes**. Helps teams show how design decisions influence product direction, business goals, and design impact.

**Measure** — The Decision Map area focused on **understanding how the work performs**. Helps teams choose the UX metrics and input needed to strengthen a signal.

**Participant Input** — Responses from people who interact with a concept, flow, message, or experience. Helps create or strengthen signals by showing how real users respond.

**Prompt** — A one-time instruction used with an AI tool. Useful for generating ideas, reframing questions, creating variations, exploring directions quickly. **Prompts are flexible, but they are temporary.** Skills make that work repeatable.

**Signal Loop** — The repeatable flow of turning a hunch into a signal and applying it to a decision. Start with a hunch -> connect to user needs and business goals -> anchor to a UX metric -> apply to a decision -> carry the signal forward.

**Tradeoff** — What the team gains or gives up choosing one direction over another. Signals make tradeoffs easier to see because they show how different options perform against what matters.

**User Need** — What a user is trying to do, understand, feel, or accomplish. Gives the signal its **human anchor**. Without a clear user need, signals drift toward internal opinion or business-only goals.

**UX Metric** — A measure that helps the team understand how well the experience is working. Common UX metrics: comprehension, usability, confidence, desirability, trust, completion. Turns user input into signals that can guide decisions.

### Most common term-pair confusions

| Confusion | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Prompt vs. Skill | Prompt = once. Skill = repeatable. |
| Hunch vs. Signal | Hunch = early read. Signal = hunch shaped with UX metric + user need + business goal. |
| Signal vs. UX metric | UX metric = measurement. Signal = metric + intuition + direction + context + user need + business goal. |
| Agent vs. Skill | Agent = manages goal and chooses Skills. Skill = does the repeatable step. |
| Decision Map vs. Design Assessment | Map = how a single decision takes shape. Assessment = how decisions move through the org over time. |
| Decision Map vs. Design Review | Map = the four areas decisions evolve through. Review = the moment signals get applied to land a decision. |
| Define / Measure / Focus / Lead | Areas of the Decision Map. Not stages — teams move back and forth. |

### Five-dimension quick reference (Design Assessment)

| Dimension | Diagnoses |
|---|---|
| Organizing Work | Is work structured clearly, or scattered? |
| Managing Complexity | Can teams handle multiple directions, or does complexity slow decisions? |
| Building Proof | Are signals strong and consistent, or is work running on assumption? |
| Guiding Decisions | Are signals actually used, or do conversations drift? |
| Scaling Influence | Do decisions carry across teams and time, or do they get revisited? |

## Critical Rules

- Use canonical phrasing. Don't paraphrase named concepts.
- A **prompt** is one-time. A **Skill** is repeatable. Never blur them.
- A **hunch** is an early read. A **signal** is a shaped, anchored hunch. Never call a hunch a signal.
- A **UX metric** is a measurement. A **signal** is the metric plus intuition, direction, context, user need, business goal. Don't reduce signals to metrics.
- The Decision Map's four areas (Define / Measure / Focus / Lead) are **not stages**. Teams move back and forth.
- Definitions only. If they want to use the concept, route them.

**The goal is to leave the user with a precise, canonical definition they can drop into a doc, prompt, or conversation without losing meaning.**
