# Align Ownership — Reference

Compressed source: Glare | Design Review, Aligning Techniques v1.3 + the A row of the Rubric and Scoring Model.

## Intent

Create clarity around responsibility and boundaries. Connect the decision to a person and a role.

## When to use

- When the user is making ownership visible before a design review ends.
- When everyone agreed on the direction but no one owns the execution.
- When responsibility is blurry — who's leading vs supporting vs reviewing vs approving.
- When Align scored 1–2 on the SIGNAL rubric.
- When prepping the closing minutes of a review or kickoff where execution is unclear.
- When scope keeps creeping because boundaries weren't set.

**Don't use when** no decision has been made yet (use `glare-review-navigate`) or when ownership is clear but next steps are vague (use `glare-review-lock`).

## Why it matters

Ambiguity creates drift. When ownership is unclear, accountability weakens and complexity increases.

When Align is weak:
- people assume someone else is taking action
- responsibilities overlap
- follow-through depends on people who were not included
- decisions lose momentum
- the same topic comes back later

When Align is strong, the work moves forward more easily. Everyone knows what is expected and who is responsible.

## What it considers

- whether a clear owner is identified
- whether decision authority is understood
- whether responsibilities are defined

## What to listen for

- clear naming of owners
- confirmation of responsibility
- clarity on who is driving the next step

## What this unlocks

Ownership creates accountability and helps the work move forward.

## What breaks if it's weak

Work slows down. Responsibility is assumed but not confirmed.

## Connection to the next step

Momentum depends on ownership.

## Scoring (1–5)

| Score | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Leading | Clear ownership and clean boundaries |
| 4 | Advancing | Most ownership explicit, minor gaps |
| 3 | Functional | Partial clarity |
| 2 | Developing | Some ownership discussed, not confirmed |
| 1 | Reactive | Ambiguity persists |

**Metric hint:** Look at ownership statements — who committed to what, and was it explicit?

## When to use Align techniques

- people agree with the direction, but no one owns the next step
- several people are involved, but roles are blurry
- the group needs input from someone who is not in the room
- follow-through depends on coordination across people or teams
- everyone assumes someone else will take action

By the end of Align, the team should be able to say: who owns the next step, who is supporting the work, who needs to review or approve it, what expertise is still needed, what each person is responsible for.

## Technique chooser

| If… | Use… |
|---|---|
| No one is clearly responsible | Name the owner |
| Multiple people are involved | Confirm the role |
| The group is missing knowledge or authority | Bring in the right expertise |
| Ownership feels soft | Restate responsibility |

## The four techniques

### 1. Name the owner

When no one is clearly responsible, progress stalls. People assume someone else will take the next step. Naming the owner removes that ambiguity.

- **Watch for:** unclear next steps; hesitation after the call; lack of follow-through.
- **What it does:** creates accountability; removes ambiguity; helps the work move forward.
- **Example:** "Who's taking this forward from here?"

### 2. Confirm the role

Ownership is not always simple. Someone may be involved, but it may not be clear whether they are leading, supporting, or reviewing.

- **Watch for:** multiple people involved without clear roles; overlapping responsibilities; confusion about who is doing what.
- **What it does:** clarifies responsibility; improves coordination; reduces friction.
- **Example:** "Are you owning this next step, or supporting it?"

### 3. Bring in the right expertise

Sometimes the people in the room are not enough to move the work forward. Identifying this early avoids delays.

- **Watch for:** uncertainty about how to move forward; missing expertise; decisions being delayed.
- **What it does:** improves decision quality; reduces rework; helps maintain momentum.
- **Example:** "Should we bring someone in here before we move forward?"

### 4. Restate responsibility

Even when ownership is discussed, it can be interpreted in different ways. A quick restatement locks in clarity.

- **Watch for:** vague agreement on ownership; different interpretations of next steps; follow-up questions after the call.
- **What it does:** reinforces clarity; prevents miscommunication; supports execution.
- **Example:** "Just to confirm, you'll take this forward and we'll review it next."

## Coaching for low Align scores

**Common failure modes:**
- Vague responsibility ("the team will handle it")
- Not identifying decision authority
- Scope creeping because boundaries aren't set

**Behavioral drills:**
- "Who on your side is the final decision-maker for ___?"
- "Let me clarify the split: we own ___, you own ___. Does that match your understanding?"
- "If new requirements come up, how should we handle scope changes?"

**Success signal:** Someone names specific people for specific responsibilities.

## Bottom line

Make responsibility clear before the conversation ends. When ownership is aligned, decisions turn into action, progress continues, and the work moves forward without unnecessary delays.

## Failure modes

These are common ways this skill goes wrong in practice:

- **"Team will own it."** No individual is accountable, so it falls to whoever has the most time — and usually doesn't get done. Name a person, not a team.
- **Owner without authority.** Naming someone responsible who can't actually move the work (no scope authority, no access to the system, no stakeholder relationship). The owner must have the room to act.
- **Skipping the "supporting" role.** Naming a primary owner without naming who reviews, approves, or unblocks. The owner stalls when missing inputs aren't anticipated.
- **Re-opening the decision in Align.** Treating Align as another round of debate about whether the call was right. Align is execution — if the decision is still in play, return to Navigate.
