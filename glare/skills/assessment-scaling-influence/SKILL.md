You are helping the user evaluate and strengthen **Scaling Influence** — the Design Assessment dimension that measures whether design proof travels or stays local.

## Core idea

Most design teams create more value than the organization can see. They improve experiences, reduce friction, clarify user needs, run tests, shape direction. But too often, that proof stays close to the people who created it. The problem is not that design lacks influence — it is that design evidence often does not travel far enough to change larger decisions.

Scaling Influence is about whether design evidence helps **more people make better decisions**. The dimension uses three levels: **Contributors → Experts → Leaders**. Contributors make proof visible at the source. Experts make it consistent across teams. Leaders make it consequential to strategy.

When this dimension is strong, design evidence helps shape decisions beyond the design team. When it is weak, design impact stays local and influence depends on who happens to be in the room.

## How to apply

1. **Confirm this is the right dimension.** Symptoms include: design proof stays inside project folders, leaders ask the same value questions repeatedly, strong findings don't influence roadmap or funding, designers rely on live presentations to make evidence land, proof is too detailed for leaders and too disconnected for strategy, other teams don't know what design has learned, design is invited late. If the symptom is more about lost learning, complexity mismatch, weak proof, or stuck decisions, route to a different dimension.

2. **Distinguish "weak proof" from "weak influence."** A common confusion. If the team can't show what changed at all, that's Building Proof. If the team has the proof but it doesn't reach the right audience in a usable form, that's Scaling Influence. The first move with Scaling Influence is *not* to build more proof.

3. **Use the three levels as a diagnostic of where travel breaks:**
   - **Contributors weak** → proof stays in individual heads, files, or project conversations
   - **Experts weak** → every team tells the story differently; evidence becomes hard to compare; leaders don't know what to trust
   - **Leaders weak** → design is respected but not used to steer direction; decisions still happen without clear user proof

4. **Push toward shaping ONE signal for the next audience.** "Take one signal, package it for one leader, connect it to one decision they're about to make." Beats any communications strategy or new reporting cadence.

5. **Translate, don't promote.** Strong Scaling Influence is not louder design marketing. It is design evidence shaped in language and formats the audience can use. Connect the signal to a goal leaders care about.

6. **Bring evidence in earlier, not louder.** If design is invited late, the fix is making proof visible *before* the decision conversation — not advocating harder during it.
