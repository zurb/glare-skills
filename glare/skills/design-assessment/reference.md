# Design Assessment — Overview

Compressed from the v1.3 master page (May 3, 2026). The Design Assessment is one of four top-level Glare components: **Get Started, Design Signals, Design Review, Decision Map (Define / Measure / Focus / Lead), Design Assessment**.

> "The goal is not to measure design. It is to make design move."

## What the assessment is

A diagnostic that shows where design impact is created inside the organization — and where it starts to break down. Design work doesn't usually fail all at once. It loses strength as it moves: clarity fades, proof gets harder to explain, decisions slow down, momentum drops.

The assessment looks beyond outputs at the *system around the work*. It shows how well your team:

- turns ideas into decisions
- turns decisions into proof
- turns proof into visible impact
- keeps learning connected across projects
- helps design influence the work around it

It is not a judgment of the team. It is a way to see where the system is strong, where it is uneven, and where a small improvement could unlock the most movement.

## When to use

- When the user wants a system-level diagnostic of design maturity — not a single-project review.
- When asking "where is design impact breaking down" or "what's our team's design maturity."
- When introducing the five dimensions (Organizing Work, Managing Complexity, Building Proof, Guiding Decisions, Scaling Influence).
- When the user names symptoms — "research never gets used," "leadership doesn't see our value," "design proof stays trapped."
- When two teams score the same total but feel like they have different problems — the dimension shape will tell you why.

**Don't use when** deep in one dimension (route to that dimension's skill), interpreting a score (`glare:assessment-scoring`, `glare:assessment-reading`), or planning improvement (`glare:assessment-using-results`).

## The five dimensions

Design impact does not break in one place. It breaks across a few repeatable failure points. Each dimension answers one question about the system:

| Dimension | What it shows | What strong looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Organizing Work** | Where design knowledge either compounds or gets lost. | The team builds a body of evidence that helps future work move faster, instead of restarting from scratch. |
| **Managing Complexity** | Where growing work either stays clear or becomes harder to move. | The team creates enough structure to keep progress moving across products, teams, systems, and decisions. |
| **Building Proof** | Where design either connects to results or stays trapped inside the team. | The team can explain what changed, why it mattered, and how the work created value. |
| **Guiding Decisions** | Where signals either create direction or get lost in debate. | The team uses signals to move decisions forward instead of circling around opinions. |
| **Scaling Influence** | Where proof either travels or stays local. | The team makes design proof visible, trusted, and useful to leaders beyond the immediate project. |

## How the assessment works

Each dimension is scored separately. The score shows how consistently design creates clarity and momentum in that area. Together, the five scores reveal the *pattern* of how design moves through the organization:

- where work starts clearly
- where decisions slow down
- where proof fades
- where evidence fails to travel
- where influence can grow

> "The score matters, but the pattern matters more."

A team can have one strong dimension and still struggle to create impact. Another team may score evenly across dimensions but need a stronger proof story. The assessment helps you see the shape of the system, not just a number.

## What the assessment is *not*

It does not measure individual design skill. It does not judge whether a designer is good, talented, creative, or capable. It measures the system: how work starts, how learning is captured, how complexity is handled, how proof is built, how decisions are made, how evidence travels.

Design impact rarely depends on talent alone. It depends on whether the organization gives design a clear path to create impact.

## What you get

After taking the assessment, the team walks away with:

- a score across each of the five dimensions
- a clearer picture of strengths and gaps
- a shared language for design maturity
- a better view of where momentum breaks
- focused next actions
- a path for improving decision quality, proof, and influence

## How to use the results

Start by looking for the biggest break in the system. Ask:

- Where are we strongest?
- Where does work slow down?
- Where does proof stop moving?
- Where do decisions get revisited?
- Where does leadership lose visibility?
- What can we improve in the next 30 days?

Then choose **one dimension** to strengthen first. Small, visible improvements build momentum faster than broad transformation plans. The point is not to fix everything at once. The point is to find the part of the system that will create the most movement.

## How this fits with the rest of Glare

- **Design Signals** are the unit of clarity the assessment measures the *flow* of. Strong signals feed every dimension.
- **Decision Map (Define / Measure / Focus / Lead)** is the operating cycle the assessment evaluates. Organizing Work explicitly uses the Decision Map blocks to trace how knowledge moves.
- **Design Review** is one place where the system gets tested in real time — weak Guiding Decisions or weak Building Proof show up most visibly in stalled review meetings.
- **Get Started** introduces the assessment as the fifth check on whether the rest of the system is holding.

## Failure modes

These are common ways this skill goes wrong in practice:

- **Using the assessment as a single-project review.** It's a system-level instrument. Running it against one launch produces a misleading snapshot — the dimensions describe how design impact moves through the organization over time, not how one project performed.
- **Skipping straight to the score.** The total is the *least* informative output; the pattern across dimensions is the diagnosis. Reading the system means reading the shape, not the number.
- **Running the assessment too often.** Re-scoring every quarter produces noise rather than insight. A 6–12 month rhythm lets the system actually change between measurements.
- **Treating it as a performance review for designers.** The assessment measures the system *around* the work — workflows, organizational habits, leadership posture. Using it to evaluate designers misreads the instrument and damages trust.

## Note on terminology

The Decision Map's fourth phase was renamed from "Show" to **Lead** in v1.3. The Design Assessment dimensions are not named by the Define / Measure / Focus / Lead axis — each dimension has its own layer structure (Organizing Work uses Objectives / Drivers / Learning / Outputs; Managing Complexity uses Simple / Complicated / Complex; Building Proof uses Research / Intent / Outcome / Impact; Guiding Decisions uses Exploratory / Operational / Analytical / Evidential; Scaling Influence uses Contributors / Experts / Leaders). See each dimension skill for its own layer model.
