# Organizing Work — Design Assessment Dimension

Compressed from "Glare | Design Assessment, Organizing Work v1.3" (May 3, 2026).

> "Design work gets weaker when teams lose the memory of the work."

## What this dimension measures

Whether design knowledge **compounds** across projects or **disappears** between meetings, files, and teams. It evaluates how well the team captures, shares, and reuses what it learns — and whether the reasoning behind decisions stays visible long enough to inform the next project.

A team strong in this dimension can clearly answer:

- What were we trying to achieve?
- What did we believe going in?
- What did we learn?
- What changed because of that learning?
- Where can this evidence be reused?
- How does this work help the next decision?

## Why it matters

Most teams have plenty of evidence. The problem is that evidence gets scattered. When that happens:

- teams repeat the same debates
- decisions lose context
- new work starts without past learning
- findings stay trapped in old decks
- strong signals do not shape future direction
- leaders cannot see how design thinking has developed

The work may be good, but the learning does not carry forward.

## Where momentum breaks

Momentum breaks when the team cannot easily trace the work. A project may start with a clear goal, but the reasoning gets lost as more people get involved. A test produces strong findings, but no one knows where to find them three months later. A design decision is smart, but the next team cannot see what evidence supported it.

You see weak Organizing Work when:

- research findings are hard to find later
- teams rerun similar studies without knowing it
- design decisions are explained differently by different people
- stakeholders ask the same questions again
- new team members lack context
- evidence does not connect across projects
- past learning does not influence new work

The issue is not effort. It is the lack of a clear memory system for design.

## What strong looks like

Strong Organizing Work makes design learning easier to carry forward. The team captures the purpose of the work, the reasoning behind decisions, the signals collected, and outputs others can use. Evidence does not sit in one person's head or one project folder. It becomes part of how the organization learns.

Strong teams:

- define the goal before the work begins
- document the hunches and assumptions behind the work
- capture signals as the work progresses
- connect findings to decisions
- turn learning into simple, reusable outputs
- make evidence easy for other teams to understand
- build a stronger foundation for future work

This does not mean creating a heavy documentation process. It means making the important parts of the work easier to see.

## What weak looks like

Weak Organizing Work creates repeated friction. The team may be moving fast, but the learning does not stick. Each project feels separate. Decisions depend on whoever was in the room. Evidence exists, but it is not easy to use.

Weak teams often:

- start projects without clear goals
- lose track of why decisions were made
- capture findings without turning them into action
- store research in places others do not use
- create decks that are read once and forgotten
- struggle to connect past learning to new work
- depend on individual memory instead of shared evidence

This makes design feel less mature, even when the work itself is strong.

## How it is evaluated — the four layers

Organizing Work is evaluated through the **Decision Map**. The Decision Map gives the team a shared way to track how design knowledge moves through the work, across the Glare blocks. The assessment focuses on four layers:

```
Objectives → Drivers → Learning → Outputs
```

These layers show whether the team is connecting the right inputs, decisions, evidence, and results — and whether design knowledge is being carried from the start of the work into something others can understand, trust, and reuse.

### Layer 1 — Objectives

How well the team uses the Decision Map to define **what the work is trying to achieve**. Includes blocks like user needs, concepts, initiatives, and business goals. Together they explain why the work exists and what success should look like.

- **Strong:** the team can clearly show the connection between the user problem, the design direction, and the business goal.
- **Weak:** work starts broadly. Teams create ideas, tests, or deliverables without a clear way to explain what the work is meant to change.

### Layer 2 — Drivers

How well the team captures **what is pushing the work forward**. Includes blocks like audience, hunches, methods, and workflows. These explain who the work is for, what the team believes, how they plan to learn, and how the work fits into how the team operates.

- **Strong:** the team can explain why it chose a direction, method, or audience.
- **Weak:** decisions feel disconnected. The team may know what it did, but not why that path made sense.

### Layer 3 — Learning

How well the team captures **what it discovers as the work moves**. Includes blocks like collecting, questioning, comparing, and mapping. These help the team gather signals, ask sharper questions, compare options, and connect evidence across the system.

- **Strong:** the team can see how evidence changed the work.
- **Weak:** signals stay scattered across notes, tests, conversations, and dashboards. The team has information, but the learning does not become easy to use.

### Layer 4 — Outputs

How well the team turns learning into **something others can act on**. Includes blocks like UX metrics, findings, decisions, and results. These show what was measured, what was learned, what choice was made, and what changed because of it.

- **Strong:** evidence becomes clear, reusable, and leadership-ready.
- **Weak:** learning stays local. Valuable work does not travel far enough to shape future decisions.

## How to strengthen Organizing Work

Start by making the reasoning easier to follow. You do not need a massive repository or a new operating model. Use the Decision Map to trace one project from the first objective to the final output.

The goal is to create a clearer trail:

```
Objective → Driver → Learning → Output
```

That trail should show what the team was trying to achieve, what shaped the direction, what signals changed the work, and what others can reuse later.

**Useful moves:**

- define the goal before the work starts
- connect the user need to the business goal
- write down the hunches behind the direction
- clarify the audience, method, and workflow
- capture the signals that shaped the work
- summarize findings in plain language
- connect each finding to a decision
- create one reusable artifact others can reference
- store evidence where the next team can find it

The goal is not documentation for its own sake. It is to make design learning easier to understand, trust, and reuse.

## Diagnostic questions

Use these to evaluate how well your team organizes design work:

- Can someone quickly understand why this work started?
- Are the user need and business goal clear?
- Did we capture the hunches and assumptions behind the work?
- Can we see what signals changed our thinking?
- Are findings connected to decisions?
- Is there a clear output others can reuse?
- Can another team apply what we learned?
- Will this work still make sense three months from now?

## What improves when this is strong

When Organizing Work is strong, design becomes easier to scale:

- teams spend less time reconstructing context
- leaders see how decisions formed
- new work builds on past learning
- research and design evidence become more valuable because they don't disappear after one project
- repeated debates reduce
- onboarding gets faster
- evidence is reused across projects
- decisions are easier to explain
- a shared language for design impact forms

This is where design maturity starts to compound.

## How this dimension supports the others

When design knowledge is easier to trace, teams can:

- **manage complexity** with more context
- **build proof** from stronger evidence
- **guide decisions** with less debate
- **scale influence** because the story is easier to share

## Smallest-leverage next move

Pick one recent project and trace it through the Decision Map: Objective → Driver → Learning → Output. Find the weakest link. That is where Organizing Work should improve first.

Do **not** start by building a research repository or a documentation standard. Start with one trail. One project. One reusable artifact.

## Note on terminology

The Decision Map's fourth phase was renamed from "Show" to **Lead** in v1.3. Organizing Work uses Decision Map blocks but does not name its layers by Define / Measure / Focus / Lead. Its four layers (Objectives, Drivers, Learning, Outputs) span across Decision Map blocks rather than mapping one-to-one onto the Define / Measure / Focus / Lead phases.
