# Focus — Compressed (v1.3)

## What Focus is

Focus is the third facet of the Glare Decision Map. Measure turns hunches into questions and findings. Focus takes those findings and helps teams decide what deserves more attention, what should be compared, and what direction is strong enough to move forward.

Focus is where teams stop treating every idea the same. A design may be understandable, but that does not mean it is the best option. A concept may test well on its own, but that does not mean it beats another direction, works across audiences, or creates enough pull to justify investment. Focus helps teams look at the work in context.

It brings structure to how teams run initiatives, choose methods, compare results, and make decisions. The goal is not to test everything — it is to learn enough to put energy behind the right work.

## When to use

- When the user has findings and needs to turn them into direction.
- When circling, scattered findings, or the team can't pick what to commit to.
- When working through Focus's four moves — Initiatives → Methods → Comparing → Decisions.
- When the user mentions the five tactical decision types (Implement / Refine Design / Test Iteration / Revisit Later / Do Not Pursue).
- When narrowing scattered concepts into one clear direction the team can defend.

**Don't use when** still gathering findings (use `glare-measure`), defining the problem (use `glare-define`), or escalating to leadership KPIs (use `glare-lead`). For depth in one move, use `glare-focus-initiatives`, `glare-focus-methods`, `glare-focus-comparing`, or `glare-focus-decisions`.

## The four moves

Focus works through four moves, in order:

1. **Initiatives** — Define the area of work that needs attention. Without a clear initiative, signals scatter across too many ideas.
2. **Methods** — Choose the best way to learn. The right method matches the question, the risk, and the decision the team needs to make.
3. **Comparing** — Place signals side by side. Compare versions, audiences, competitors, or time periods to see what is stronger, weaker, or worth changing.
4. **Decisions** — Turn evidence into action. Make a clear choice, explain the tradeoff, and move the work forward with confidence.

Focus is more than picking a winner. It sharpens design direction, tests desirability, and turns scattered signals into decisions the team can act on.

## What goes into Focus

Focus works best when the team has something real to evaluate. It usually starts with:
- an initiative that needs direction
- a concept, flow, page, or experience to test
- a UX metric that shows what matters
- a signal from users
- a comparison point (another version, audience, competitor, or benchmark)

Without those inputs, Focus turns into preference. With them, the team can compare the work fairly and choose with more confidence.

## The four moves in detail

### Initiatives — Define the work that needs focus

Most design work gets messy when the team does not agree on what is being improved. Feedback spreads across too many problems, priorities compete, and no one knows what the work is meant to change. Initiatives cut through.

They give the team a clear area of focus — a product flow, page, feature, campaign, or journey. A strong initiative connects user needs to business goals so the team knows where to look, what to measure, and why the work matters.

- Anchor the work in a clear opportunity.
- Connect the initiative to user and business needs.
- Keep the scope tight enough to learn quickly.
- Decide what improvement would make the work stronger.

### Methods — Choose the right way to learn

Most teams lose time when they use the wrong method for the decision in front of them. Simple questions get over-tested, risky ideas get under-tested, and the team ends up with answers that do not move the work. Methods cut through.

They give the team a structured way to collect the right signal at the right moment. A strong method matches the question, the risk, and the type of decision the team needs to make.

- Match the method to the question.
- Use the simplest path to get a trustworthy signal.
- Balance speed, depth, and confidence.
- Create a repeatable way to learn across projects.

### Comparing — See what performs better

Most decisions stall when teams look at results in isolation. One score feels useful, but no one knows if it is strong, weak, or better than the alternative. Comparing cuts through.

It places signals side by side so teams can see what changed, what improved, and what direction creates more pull. A strong comparison gives context to the data and makes tradeoffs easier to explain.

- Compare versions with the same metrics.
- Look across audiences, devices, and moments.
- Use benchmarks to understand progress over time.
- Find the direction with the strongest signal.

### Decisions — Turn evidence into action

Most teams do not struggle because they lack ideas. They struggle because they do not know when to commit. Findings sit in decks, options stay open, meetings circle the same debate. Decisions cut through.

They turn signals into a clear choice the team can act on. A strong decision explains what was chosen, what was traded off, and why the evidence supports moving forward.

- Name the choice clearly.
- Connect the decision to the strongest signal.
- Explain the tradeoff behind the direction.
- Move the work forward with confidence.

## How Focus works

Focus moves teams from learning to choosing. It starts with an initiative — the area of work where a better decision would create value (product flow, campaign, feature, key journey moment).

Then the team moves through four simple steps:
1. Name the initiative
2. Choose the method
3. Compare the signal
4. Make the decision

The goal is not to over-test everything. It is to use the simplest method that creates a trustworthy signal. Once the signal is captured, the team compares it across:
- Versions
- Audiences
- Moments
- Benchmarks

That comparison shows what is stronger, what is weaker, and where the work is losing force. Then the team makes the decision: commit to a direction, explain the tradeoff, and move forward. Focus does not make the process heavier — it makes the decision easier to trust.

## What Focus improves

When Focus is working well:
- Teams know which work deserves attention.
- Research is tied to real choices, not just general learning.
- Comparisons are easy to explain because the team can show why one direction is stronger.
- Decisions move faster — stakeholders spend less time circling the same debate.
- Design feels connected to progress the team can see.

Focus helps teams:
- See where to put energy
- Compare options with the same metrics
- Explain why one direction is stronger
- Reduce second-guessing
- Connect design work to measurable progress

Focus narrows the work without narrowing the thinking. It keeps teams open while learning, then decisive when the signal is clear.

## What comes out of Focus

A clear decision. A strong decision explains:
- what the team chose
- which signal supported the choice
- what tradeoff was made
- what should happen next

This keeps the work from stalling after research is done. The team does not just learn something — it moves.

## Where Focus fits in Glare

Focus sits between Measure and Lead in the Decision Map.

- **Define** clarifies what matters.
- **Measure** tests what works.
- **Focus** decides what to do.
- **Lead** shows what it drives.

Focus is the turning point where signals become choices, and choices become momentum.

## Failure modes

These are common ways this skill goes wrong in practice:

- **Skipping straight to Decisions.** Teams under deadline pressure compress Initiatives, Methods, and Comparing into one meeting and call the move "the decision." It isn't — without framing and comparison, the call is opinion in a faster wrapper.
- **Comparing before framing the initiative.** "Which version is better?" without naming what the initiative is for produces winners no one trusts. Initiative comes first.
- **Letting Focus turn into another round of Measure.** When findings feel weak, the impulse is to gather more data. Focus exists to *use* findings; if the team keeps cycling back to Measure, name what specific finding is missing rather than re-running everything.
- **No decision record at the end.** A Focus cycle that produces alignment but no written decision leaks the same call into the next meeting. Lock the decision into a one-line record before moving on.
