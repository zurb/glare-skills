## Why teams need support now

Most teams are shipping, reviewing, testing, iterating. AI adds even more motion. Prompts create new directions quickly. Engineers move faster. Teams can explore more options than before.

**The harder part is keeping the work connected.**

As output increases, teams need a stronger way to answer:
- what are we actually deciding?
- what signal tells us this direction is stronger?
- how does this connect to user needs?
- how does it support a business goal?
- how do we carry the decision forward?

Without that structure, teams end up with more output, more feedback, and more meetings, but not always stronger decisions. **Coaching helps leaders build the rhythm needed to keep the work moving with clarity.**

## AI Workflows in coaching

AI makes it easier to generate ideas, variations, and concepts. That speed can help teams explore more, but can also create more confusion when there's no shared way to evaluate output.

Coaching helps teams use AI Skills in a structured way. AI Skills support the work by helping teams:
- frame questions as decisions
- clarify user needs
- connect work to business goals
- shape concepts into something comparable
- interpret signals consistently

This gives product, design, engineering, and AI systems a shared way to move from output to direction. **The point is not to use AI for more production alone. The point is to use AI to accelerate the decision loop.**

## How Glare works inside your team (the four components, applied)

### Design Signals
Coaching helps teams create stronger signals by connecting:
- UX metrics
- intuition
- direction
- context
- user needs
- business goals

A signal gives the team a shared reference point. It shows what users are revealing and how that should shape the decision.

### Decision Map
Coaching helps teams see how decisions move through the work. The Decision Map shows where signals need to be strengthened across:
- Define
- Measure
- Focus
- Lead

This helps leaders see where work is getting stuck and what information is needed to move forward.

### Design Review
Coaching helps teams turn reviews into decision moments. Most reviews create more feedback than direction. Coaching helps leaders bring signals into the room so conversations stay focused, tradeoffs are visible, and decisions land.

### Design Assessment
Coaching helps teams see where the system is breaking. The assessment looks across five dimensions:
- Organizing Work
- Managing Complexity
- Building Proof
- Guiding Decisions
- Scaling Influence

This shows where signals are strong, where decisions lose clarity, and where leaders should focus next.

## Create stronger signals inside real work

A signal usually starts with a hunch. Something feels unclear. A direction feels stronger. A flow might create friction.

Coaching helps teams take that hunch and shape it into a signal that can guide a real decision. That means clarifying:
- what the team believes
- what users need
- what business goal matters
- what UX metric should be watched
- what direction is being considered
- what decision needs to be made

Instead of debating from separate perspectives, the team looks at the same signal and decides what moves forward.

---

## Prove design impact with signals

Most teams can show the work. The harder part is showing what changed because of the work. Coaching helps leaders build that connection.

The chain leaders learn to articulate:

**decision → signal → user behavior → product outcome → business impact**

Instead of saying "we think this direction is better," teams can explain:
- what decision was made
- what signal supported it
- what changed for users
- what outcome it is expected to influence
- what should happen next

This helps design move from presentation to proof. (See `glare-proving-design-impact` for the full play.)

## The 90-Day Coaching & Advising Pilot

The best way to start: **one team, one initiative, one signal.**

A focused 90-day pilot gives leaders enough time to apply the framework, build the rhythm, and show visible progress without turning it into a large transformation program.

### Month 1: Establish the signal

Start with one high-leverage initiative. Together, clarify:
- the business goal
- the user need
- the decision that needs to be made
- the UX metric that can help read the work
- the first signal loop

**Goal:** create the first usable signal and show how it can guide a real decision.

### Month 2: Build the rhythm

Coach leaders through the moments where decisions usually fall apart:
- design reviews
- stakeholder conversations
- signal interpretation
- quality measurement
- decision framing

**Goal:** make signals part of how the team works, not something that sits outside the process.

### Month 3: Scale the model

Turn what worked into a repeatable model:
- a quality scorecard
- signal patterns
- decision review practices
- executive-ready proof stories
- a roadmap for scaling across more teams

**Goal:** help leaders keep using the system after the pilot ends.
