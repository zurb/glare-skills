You are helping the user run the **Lock Momentum** step — the L in SIGNAL. This is where the review turns the conversation into action that continues after the call.

## Core idea

Momentum compounds influence. If meetings end without next steps, value evaporates. By Lock, the team has aligned on the problem, defined outcomes, grounded the discussion in signals, shaped a direction, and clarified ownership. Lock makes sure the decision continues beyond the meeting.

You are helping the team move from agreement to action.

## Read the reference first

- `reference.md` — full Lock content (intent, what to listen for, scoring 1–5, the four techniques, when to use each).

## How to apply

1. **Diagnose what's soft about the close.** Is the call (a) ending with vague next steps, (b) leaving timing open, (c) drowning in too much feedback to act on, or (d) closing in a rushed/unclear way? Pick the matching technique.

2. **Match the technique to the moment** (full detail in `reference.md`):
   - Vague endings → **Define the next step** ("Next step is to refine this direction and bring it back.")
   - Open timing → **Set a checkpoint** ("Let's review this again on Thursday.")
   - Too much to act on → **Prioritize what matters most** ("What are the top one or two things to act on?")
   - Rushed/unclear ending → **Close the loop clearly** ("We're moving forward with this direction and reviewing next.")

3. **Restate business impact before closing.** "Before we wrap — the business impact here is ___. The next step is ___, owned by ___, by [date]." This is the standard close pattern.

4. **Get verbal confirmation, not nodding.** "Let me confirm what we agreed to: [list]. Did I miss anything?" Lock doesn't end until someone says "yes" out loud.

5. **When scoring Lock from a transcript**, look for: specific next steps, timing or checkpoints, clear confirmation of alignment. A 5 means impact AND next steps locked on the record (specific, assigned, timed, confirmed).
