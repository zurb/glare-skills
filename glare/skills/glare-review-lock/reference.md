# Lock Momentum — Reference

Compressed source: Glare | Design Review, Lock Momentum v1.3 + the L row of the Rubric and Scoring Model.

## Intent

Convert insight into forward motion. Make sure the decision continues beyond the meeting.

## Why it matters

Momentum compounds influence. If meetings end without next steps, value evaporates.

When Lock is weak:
- next steps are vague or unclear
- people use "we'll follow up" language
- timing is not confirmed
- too many things feel important
- the same conversation needs to happen again

When Lock is strong, the work continues right away. The team knows what to do next, who is doing it, and when to reconnect.

## What it considers

- whether actions are clearly defined
- whether timing is established
- whether alignment is confirmed

## What to listen for

- specific next steps
- timing or checkpoints
- clear confirmation of alignment

## What this unlocks

Momentum carries the decision forward and turns agreement into action.

## What breaks if it's weak

The call ends without traction. The same topics return later.

## Connection to the system

This completes the cycle and sets up the next conversation.

## Scoring (1–5)

| Score | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Leading | Impact and next steps locked |
| 4 | Advancing | Next steps defined with owners, timeline soft |
| 3 | Functional | Next steps soft |
| 2 | Developing | Vague follow-up mentioned |
| 1 | Reactive | Call ended without commitment |

**Metric hint:** Look at next steps in evidence — were they specific, assigned, and confirmed?

## When to use Lock techniques

- next steps are vague
- people use "we'll follow up" language
- timing is unclear
- too many things feel important
- the group agrees, but action is not confirmed
- the same discussion may need to happen again

By the end of Lock, the team should be able to say: what happens next, who is doing it, when it will happen, what matters most, when the group will reconnect, how progress will continue.

## Technique chooser

| If… | Use… |
|---|---|
| The next action is unclear | Define the next step |
| Timing is open-ended | Set a checkpoint |
| There is too much feedback to act on | Prioritize what matters most |
| The ending feels rushed or unclear | Close the loop clearly |

## The four techniques

### 1. Define the next step

Even when a decision feels clear, it can stall if the next step is not defined.

- **Watch for:** vague endings; "we'll follow up" language; uncertainty after the call.
- **What it does:** turns alignment into action; keeps progress moving; prevents rework.
- **Example:** "Next step is to refine this direction and bring it back."

### 2. Set a checkpoint

Momentum needs a clear point to reconnect. Without a checkpoint, work drifts.

- **Watch for:** open-ended timelines; lack of follow-up; slow or uneven progress.
- **What it does:** maintains accountability; keeps work on track; supports ongoing alignment.
- **Example:** "Let's review this again on Thursday."

### 3. Prioritize what matters most

Feedback sessions often generate more ideas than can be acted on right away.

- **Watch for:** long lists of feedback; hesitation after the call; lack of clear focus.
- **What it does:** reduces overwhelm; creates focus; improves execution.
- **Example:** "What are the top one or two things to act on?"

### 4. Close the loop clearly

How the conversation ends matters. If unclear, people leave with different understandings of what was decided.

- **Watch for:** unclear or rushed endings; different interpretations of decisions; follow-up confusion.
- **What it does:** reinforces alignment; prevents rework; carries momentum forward.
- **Example:** "We're moving forward with this direction and reviewing next."

## Coaching for low Lock scores

**Common failure modes:**
- Ending without restating impact
- Vague next steps ("we'll follow up")
- No timeline or owner on actions

**Behavioral drills:**
- "Before we wrap — the business impact here is ___. The next step is ___, owned by ___, by [date]."
- "Let me confirm what we agreed to: [list]. Did I miss anything?"
- "Can we lock [date] for the next session? I'll send a calendar invite with the agenda."

**Success signal:** Someone confirms next steps with specifics, not "sounds good."

## The standard close pattern

1. Restate impact
2. Name the next step
3. Assign owner
4. Set date/checkpoint
5. Get verbal confirmation

If any one of these is missing, Lock is not done.

## Bottom line

Make sure the conversation leads to action. When momentum is locked, the work continues, progress is clear, and the team does not need to repeat the same discussion.
