# Design Review Techniques — Reference

Compressed source: Glare | Design Review, Techniques v1.3 (overview) + the four-technique sets from each dimension doc.

## Why techniques matter

Design reviews can feel productive and still lead to weak outcomes. That happens when:

- feedback becomes scattered
- people react before clarifying the problem
- opinions repeat without changing direction
- decisions take too long or do not stick
- ownership stays unclear
- next steps are vague

Techniques are simple ways to bring the conversation back. You are responding to what is happening in the room.

## How techniques work

Used in the moment. Notice where the conversation is losing strength, then pick a matching move. The output is a shift in the conversation — confusion to clarity, reaction to direction, agreement to action.

## Stuck-moment chooser

| If the room is… | Go to… |
|---|---|
| Reacting too quickly to the design | Surface |
| Sharing vague feedback ("better," "cleaner") | Identify |
| Looping on opinion, defending preferences | Ground |
| Exploring without choosing | Navigate |
| Aligned but no one owns the next step | Align |
| About to end without action | Lock |

## All 24 techniques at a glance

### S — Surface Challenges

| Technique | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Set context before feedback begins | Room lacks background | "Here's what we explored, why we made these choices, and where we're still unsure." |
| Reframe the request into the problem | People are reacting to design | "Before we react to this, we're trying to reduce drop-off at this step." |
| Ask what is not working today | The issue feels vague | "Where is this breaking down right now?" |
| Make the consequence visible | Urgency is low | "If this doesn't improve, what happens?" |

### I — Identify Outcomes

| Technique | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Define what success looks like | Success unclear | "If this works, users should move through this step without hesitation." |
| Turn feedback into an outcome | Scattered suggestions | "So the goal here is to make this easier to understand at first glance?" |
| Ask targeted questions | Feedback too broad | "Does this interaction make sense on mobile?" |
| Align on a single priority | Too many goals competing | "What's the one thing we need to get right here?" |

### G — Ground in Signals

| Technique | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Bring in one clear signal | Opinions repeating | "In testing, users hesitated at this step before continuing." |
| Compare to a baseline | Can't tell if it's better | "This version improves on where users got stuck in the earlier flow." |
| Ask for evidence behind feedback | Strong but unsupported claims | "What makes you say that?" |
| Pause when a signal shifts the conversation | Signal mentioned but ignored | "Given that, does this still feel like the right direction?" |

### N — Navigate Decisions

| Technique | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| State a clear recommendation | Conversation staying open | "Based on what we've seen, we recommend moving forward with this approach." |
| Frame the tradeoffs | People hesitate | "This simplifies the experience, but reduces flexibility here." |
| Narrow the options | Too many ideas competing | "Between these two directions, which better solves the problem we outlined?" |
| Check for agreement | Agreement assumed | "Does this direction make sense to move forward with?" |

### A — Align Ownership

| Technique | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Name the owner | No one is responsible | "Who's taking this forward from here?" |
| Confirm the role | Roles overlap | "Are you owning this next step, or supporting it?" |
| Bring in the right expertise | Missing knowledge/authority | "Should we bring someone in here before we move forward?" |
| Restate responsibility | Soft ownership | "Just to confirm, you'll take this forward and we'll review it next." |

### L — Lock Momentum

| Technique | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Define the next step | Vague endings | "Next step is to refine this direction and bring it back." |
| Set a checkpoint | Open timing | "Let's review this again on Thursday." |
| Prioritize what matters most | Too much to act on | "What are the top one or two things to act on?" |
| Close the loop clearly | Rushed/unclear ending | "We're moving forward with this direction and reviewing next." |

## How to choose

1. Notice what is weakening the conversation right now.
2. Map it to one SIGNAL letter using the chooser table above.
3. Pick the matching technique.
4. Use it once. See if the conversation shifts.
5. If not, the issue may be earlier in the SIGNAL flow — back up one letter.

## Why this improves reviews

Techniques help teams shape the conversation while it is happening. Over time, this creates more consistent design reviews, clearer decisions, and stronger outcomes.
