# Get Started — Compressed (v1.3)

## What this section is about

The Get Started section is the on-ramp into Glare, ZURB's open decision framework for product and design teams. Its job is to introduce *what Glare is* (a framework that turns scattered inputs into design signals connecting decision + measurable user response + outcome), *why it exists* (AI has accelerated output past confidence — teams can now generate faster than they can decide), and *how to start using it* through four connected components: Design Signals, Decision Map, Design Review, Design Assessment. The section delivers an introduction, a how-it-works tour, a first-signal walkthrough, AI workflow guidance, coaching/advising paths, an impact-proving playbook, a glossary, plus a personal note from founder Bryan and an open invitation to contribute.

## Introduction (v1.3)

### What Glare is

A **decision framework for product and design**, built around the observation that teams can now generate work faster than they can decide what to do with it. AI, prompts, and modern tools made flows, interfaces, and variations cheap to produce. Engineering can move from concept to implementation quickly. The bottleneck shifted: work now depends on how quickly that output can be **understood, evaluated, and turned into a decision that moves forward with confidence**.

Most teams already have the inputs:
- Designers bring intuition
- Researchers bring early signals
- Product and engineering bring constraints and goals

These inputs are valuable but rarely come together as direction. **Glare brings structure to that moment** by turning inputs into design signals.

A **design signal** connects:
- a decision the team needs to make
- a measurable user response
- the outcome that decision is meant to influence

### Where AI Skills come in

Skills are small, repeatable ways of shaping work so it can move faster with clarity. They can be used by designers, PMs, engineers, or embedded directly into prompts so teams and AI systems operate from the same structure. The continuous loop:

- prompts and tools generate options
- Skills shape those options into something testable
- signals capture how users respond
- teams use those signals to decide what moves forward

### Why Glare exists

Product/design slows at the point a team needs to decide what to do next. A concept is introduced, feedback comes in, something feels right and something feels off — but it's hard to explain why in a way the whole team can align around. Different parts of the team work from different inputs (intuitive judgment, broader insights, constraints, outcomes/risk) and those inputs don't naturally resolve into a clear decision.

**Quotes used in v1.3:**
- Jake Johnson: "We'll have a solid idea, plenty of discussion, and then someone asks what the next step is. That's usually where things fall apart because no one feels confident enough to say, 'this is the direction.'"
- Tim Banks: "The biggest challenge to measuring impact is the typical length of the feedback loop… so to shorten the loop we compromise with a mix of progress metrics and data from user studies to evaluate potential impact early."
- Ian Batterbee: "AI has made it easy to generate work quickly, but much harder to know what to trust."

Without structure: concepts expand without comparison, feedback accumulates without direction, output grows without shared understanding. Glare addresses that friction by giving teams and systems a shared structure for framing inputs, creating signals, and making decisions.

### Problems Glare solves

- data is messy or disconnected
- decisions drift toward opinion instead of evidence
- design impact is difficult to show clearly
- teams struggle to align on real user needs
- work moves quickly, but confidence lags behind

### What makes Glare work — the components

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| **Design Signals** | The unit of clarity. Connects decision + measurable user response + outcome. |
| **AI Skills and Prompts** | The repeatable structure that turns inputs into signals and signals into decisions; usable by people or embedded in prompts. |
| **The Decision Map** | Organizes decision activity into Define / Measure / Focus / Lead — not stages, but how decisions evolve through real work. |
| **Design Review** | Where signals are applied; the moment most teams lose leverage. Signals focus attention, make tradeoffs visible, help decisions land. |
| **Design Assessment** | Shows how well the system is working; surfaces where signals are strong, where they break down, how decisions move across the org. |

### How it comes together (the loop)

- a prompt or question introduces an idea
- an AI Skill shapes that idea into something testable
- a signal captures how users respond
- Design Review uses that signal to guide a decision
- the Decision Map connects that decision to outcomes
- the Design Assessment shows how well the system is holding

This loop can run within a single meeting, a test cycle, or a larger initiative.

### What this produces

- a clear signal tied to real user behavior
- a decision the team can align around
- visible tradeoffs between options
- a next step that can be executed immediately
- a direct connection between design and business outcomes

### Where this works best vs. where it needs support

| Where Glare works best | Where it needs support |
|---|---|
| Decisions are unclear or evolving | Access to users or meaningful feedback |
| Multiple options need to be compared | Shared understanding of metrics |
| User behavior needs to inform direction | Consistent use of signals in decisions |
| Teams are moving quickly and need structure | (when missing, signals are harder to create and easier to ignore) |

### Who Glare is for

| Role | How signals help | How they apply Skills | What they need |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Design Leaders** | Show how design choices influence behavior and outcomes; defensible direction | Structure evaluations, compare directions, communicate results consistently | Connect design to business outcomes; align with product/eng; show influence |
| **Product Leaders** | Reveal which directions are working; move from discussion to decision | Frame decisions, evaluate options, maintain alignment in roadmap/planning | Evaluate multiple directions; reduce roadmap debate; tie behavior to priorities |
| **Engineers / Technical Teams** | Clearer inputs on what to build and why; less ambiguity mid-stream | Use Skills inside prompts/workflows to structure ideas and evaluate early | Reduce shifting requirements; clearer decision inputs; fewer mid-stream changes |
| **Research Leaders** | Connect findings directly to decisions in the moment they matter | Structure research outputs so they're applicable in fast-moving conversations | Shorten insight-to-action distance; usable findings; shape what happens next |
| **Founders / Executives** | Fast, directional evidence on how ideas may perform before committing | Frame key questions, evaluate options quickly | Prioritize with confidence; evaluate investments; manage risk |
| **Designers** | Show how their work performs with users; reasoning visible and grounded | Test concepts, refine ideas, connect to measurable outcomes | Explain why a direction works; show user response; influence beyond output |

### When decisions matter most

Glare is most useful in moments that shape what moves forward:
- concept reviews
- roadmap discussions
- prioritization conversations
- feature validation
- release decisions

When clarity is missing, work continues but direction becomes unstable. Decisions get revisited, alignment drifts, momentum slows.

### Built from work

Glare was built by ZURB after 25+ years and **50,000 hours of testing** across thousands of projects. The pattern: teams that made consistent progress weren't doing more research or following stricter processes — they were better at turning insight into decisions others could understand and act on. Built from millions of Helio responses, tens of thousands of testing hours, and work across startups, scaleups, and large enterprises.

## How Glare Works (v1.3) — the four connected components

Glare connects how ideas are explored, how they're evaluated, and how decisions are carried forward. Built around four parts:

### 1. Design Signals
Everything starts with a signal. Turns "this feels right" or "this might be off" into something concrete. Connects decision + measurable user response + outcome. AI Skills work underneath, shaping how signals are created so they're consistent, not one-off.

### 2. Decision Map
Once signals exist: what do we do with them? The Map shows how decisions take shape. Not rigid; teams move back and forth.
- **Define** — What problem are we solving? What matters here?
- **Measure** — How is this performing? What are users showing us?
- **Focus** — Which direction is stronger? What should move forward?
- **Lead** — What do we commit to, and how does it connect to outcomes?

AI Skills keep evaluation consistent so signals connect to decisions the same way each time.

### 3. Design Review
Where most teams feel friction. Without structure, reviews expand and decisions don't land. Signals anchor the conversation. Strong reviews:
- attention stays on the decision
- tradeoffs are visible
- feedback connects back to signals
- the team can commit to a direction

### 4. Design Assessment
Looks at how decisions move through the system over time. Answers: are signals being created consistently? Are decisions actually landing? Is the work holding as it moves forward? Looks across **five dimensions**:

| Dimension | What it asks |
|---|---|
| Organizing Work | Is the work structured clearly, or scattered and reactive? |
| Managing Complexity | Can teams handle multiple directions and inputs, or does complexity slow decisions? |
| Building Proof | Are signals strong and consistent, or does work rely on assumption and opinion? |
| Guiding Decisions | Are signals actually used to make decisions, or do conversations drift? |
| Scaling Influence | Do decisions carry across teams and time, or get revisited and lose momentum? |

### How it comes together
- ideas are generated through prompts and conversations
- Skills shape how those ideas are evaluated
- signals show how users respond
- Design Reviews use those signals to guide decisions
- the Decision Map keeps those decisions moving
- Design Assessment shows how well it's all holding together

The loop repeats but doesn't reset — it builds.

## Create Your First Signal (v1.3)

The "first signal in one decision" walkthrough. See `glare-create-first-signal` for the full skill.

Six pieces a signal connects:
- a UX metric (what you're trying to understand or improve)
- your intuition (what the team is noticing)
- the direction you're considering
- the context the work sits in within a concept
- the business goal you're trying to move
- the user need you're trying to satisfy

Six-move arc: **Start with a Hunch → Make It Clear → Shape the Signal → Use the Signal → Decide What Moves Forward → Make Decisions Hold**.

## AI Workflows (v1.3)

How Glare works inside AI-supported teams. See `glare-ai-workflows` for the full skill.

Five roles in the workflow:
- **prompts generate** possibilities (one-time instructions; fast but temporary)
- **Skills shape** how possibilities are evaluated (reusable, versioned)
- **agents coordinate** the process (Ray is Glare's orchestrator)
- **participant input creates** design signals (real users, UX metrics)
- **teams make** decisions with signals

Rule: AI supports the work. People determine direction.

## Coaching & Advising (v1.3)

ZURB's offering for leaders applying Glare to active work. See `glare-coaching-advising` for the full skill.

The 90-day pilot:
- **Month 1: Establish the signal** — one initiative, clarify business goal + user need + decision + UX metric + first signal loop
- **Month 2: Build the rhythm** — coach through reviews, stakeholder conversations, signal interpretation, decision framing
- **Month 3: Scale the model** — quality scorecard, signal patterns, decision review practices, executive-ready proof stories, roadmap to scale

Built for: Heads of Design, Product Design Directors, Product Leaders, UX Research Leaders, DesignOps/ResearchOps, Transformation teams.

## Note from Bryan (v1.3)

Personal letter from ZURB founder. Not a skill — content reference.

Key beats:
- Most teams aren't sure where the real problem is. AI gives answers, tools suggest fixes, dashboards show activity — but underneath, people still operate in uncertainty.
- The pattern across decades at ZURB: teams that move forward aren't always the ones with the most data or the longest research cycle; they're the teams that can turn what users are showing into decisions others can understand and trust.
- Lineage: Foundation (responsive design system) → Helio (faster signals from users) → Glare (the underlying structure for decisions).
- Glare connects three things rarely in the same conversation: what users need, what the business cares about, how to measure the space between them.
- A design signal is the smallest unit of clarity. When signals are weak, work feels heavier than it should and decisions stall. When signals are strong, decisions get easier and tradeoffs become clearer.
- Erik Visnyak quote: "There is a challenge with bridging the customer demand signals at the top level with informing product roadmap decisions."
- Why now: AI made it easier to produce. Teams can create more than they can confidently evaluate. Clarity is more important, not less.
- Glare is still becoming. Meant to be used, questioned, stretched, improved.

## Contributing to Glare (v1.3)

Open invitation. Not a skill — content reference.

Five contribution paths:
1. **Use a Skill in real work** — notice what changed (faster decision framing? clearer user need? stronger signal? clearer next step?)
2. **Share a design signal** — the hunch, UX metric, user need, business goal, decision, what changed after
3. **Improve a Skill** — where it slowed down, what input was missing, what language felt unclear, where it broke
4. **Add an example** — what decision was on the table, what Skill structured the work, what signal became visible, what tradeoff changed the conversation, what was decided
5. **Push on the framework** — question terms, call out unclear parts, share where Skills break across contexts

Quotes used: Lyndon Cerejo ("A common set of metrics that Product & UX work toward together"), Obed Rosas (consistency challenge), Hermen Wijbenga (real-user feedback challenge), Mirjam Amoah (figuring out what design is supposed to achieve), Clara Soon (distributed value), Karline Segan (translating invisible work).

## Glossary highlights (v1.3)

Full list lives in `glare-glossary`. Most-load-bearing terms:

| Term | Quick definition |
|---|---|
| **Design Signal** | Smallest unit of clarity. Connects UX metric + intuition + direction + context + user need + business goal. |
| **AI Skill** | Reusable, versioned way of working. A prompt tells AI what to do once; a Skill defines how to do it repeatedly. |
| **Prompt** | One-time instruction to an AI tool. Fast but temporary. |
| **AI Agent** | AI system that manages a goal, chooses steps, applies Skills. Glare's orchestrator is **Ray**. |
| **AI Workflow** | The way prompts + Skills + agents + signals + teams operate together to move from idea to decision. |
| **Decision Map** | Define / Measure / Focus / Lead. How decisions take shape and move forward. |
| **Design Review** | The moment work is discussed and a decision is expected. Where signals get applied. |
| **Design Assessment** | Diagnoses how decisions move through the org across the five dimensions. |
| **Five dimensions** | Organizing Work, Managing Complexity, Building Proof, Guiding Decisions, Scaling Influence. |
| **Hunch** | Early read on the work, before evidence is clear. Becomes useful when shaped into a signal. |
| **Tradeoff** | What the team gains/gives up choosing one direction. Signals make tradeoffs visible. |
| **Participant Input** | Responses from real people interacting with a concept or experience. Strengthens signals. |
| **UX Metric** | Comprehension, usability, confidence, desirability, trust, completion, etc. Turns input into signal. |
| **Design Impact** | The change created by a design decision. Becomes clearer when decision connects to user need + UX metric + signal + product/business outcome. |
