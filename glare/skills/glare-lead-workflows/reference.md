# Lead — Workflows (per-function reference)

Every company runs on workflows already in motion whether design is in the room or not. Signals prove how a single user action ripples into adoption, retention, revenue, or cost savings. The Workflows bucket ships eight per-function templates — Sales, Marketing, Product, Engineering, Strategy, Operations, Finance, Legal — each with the same internal structure: top metrics, questions to ask, how to measure, where design creates lift, the function's jargon, and the Design KPI → Product KPI → Business KPI signal chain. Use the section that matches the function the signal is being handed off to. Quick Exercise and Quick Test apply to all eight.

---

## Sales

**Where Sales wins or loses.** Speed and clarity. Every extra step in a trial or demo slows the cycle and lowers win rates. Every week of delay is a week of quota missed.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Top metrics | pipeline growth, conversion rates, quota attainment |
| Where design creates lift | Turning trial friction into smooth onboarding that accelerates revenue recognition |

**Questions to ask:**
- Where do prospects stall in the sales cycle?
- Which demos or trial flows underperform?
- Which signals would help sales prove value faster?

**How to measure:** Test comprehension of demos, task success in trials, and sentiment about clarity.

**Signal chain (Design KPI → Product KPI → Business KPI):**
- task success during trial setup → trial-to-paid conversion rate → quota attainment and pipeline growth

**Focus signals (decision level):** Can users find it? (first-click) — Do they understand it? (comprehension) — Do they finish it? (completion, error rate).

**Jargon to mirror back:** pipeline, conversion rate, win rate, quota attainment, objections.
- Instead of "users struggled with onboarding," say "trial drop-offs are hurting pipeline conversion."
- Instead of "the demo was confusing," say "this flow is creating objections that lower win rate."

**Worked example:** Trial setup test showed only 40% of users could finish onboarding. Fix raised success to 75%, giving sales a clear proof point.

**Design-supported concepts:** Trial Setup Optimization · Demo Flow Redesign · Sales Collateral Testing · Pricing Page Clarity · Proof-of-Concept Experience · Feature Highlight Validation · Friction Mapping.

**Who to ask:** frontline reps (objections), sales ops (cycle time, conversion, CRM data), then up to managers and VPs.

## Marketing

**When messaging lands or misses.** Campaigns can generate clicks, but if the message is confusing or the page fails to convert, budgets vanish and CAC rises. Every unclear headline or broken landing page is wasted spend.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Top metrics | lead quality, campaign ROI, customer acquisition cost (CAC) |
| Where design creates lift | Translating user signals into sharper messaging and stronger campaign performance |

**Questions to ask:**
- Which campaigns have high clicks but low conversions?
- What messages confuse or fail to resonate?
- Which signals would give confidence before launch?

**How to measure:** Test landing page comprehension, visual appeal, and relevance.

**Signal chain:**
- comprehension of the offer → lead conversion rate → CAC and campaign ROI

**Focus signals:** Do users notice it? (attention) — Do they understand it? (comprehension) — Do they prefer it? (preference).

**Jargon to mirror back:** CTR, conversion rate, CAC, ROI, bounce rate.
- Instead of "users didn't like the design," say "this version dropped CTR by 20%."
- Instead of "the message isn't clear," say "confusion here is raising CAC."

**Worked example:** Headline comprehension test showed only 35% understood the offer. Revising copy lifted clarity to 70% and doubled conversions at launch.

**Design-supported concepts:** Landing Page Testing · Message Clarity Validation · Ad Concept Testing · Email Flow Optimization · Pricing Message Validation · Brand Sentiment Checks.

**Who to ask:** campaign managers (underperforming ads/pages), analytics teams (CTR, bounce, conversion), then up to CMOs/VPs of Marketing.

## Product

**The gap between shipping and adoption.** Features may ship fast, but if users cannot understand them or find value, usage stalls and churn rises. Every ignored feature is wasted effort.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Top metrics | feature adoption, retention, product–market fit |
| Where design creates lift | Turning feature hunches into early proof of adoption and retention |

**Questions to ask:**
- Which features are ignored or abandoned?
- Do users understand the value of new releases?
- Where do users get stuck or drop out in new flows?

**How to measure:** Track task success, comprehension, and satisfaction before and after launch.

**Signal chain:**
- task completion on new feature → adoption and retention rates → revenue expansion and reduced churn

**Focus signals:** Do users notice it? (first-click/discoverability) — Do they understand it? (comprehension) — Do they complete it? (completion, error rate).

**Jargon to mirror back:** adoption rate, retention rate, DAU/WAU, churn, PMF.
- Instead of "users struggled with the feature," say "low task success is limiting adoption."
- Instead of "feedback was mixed," say "satisfaction signals suggest weak product–market fit."

**Worked example:** New workflow showed only 30% could complete the task. After design changes, task success rose to 70% and adoption increased 2x post-launch.

**Design-supported concepts:** Feature Adoption Testing · Product–Market Fit Validation · Onboarding Flow Optimization · Release Comparison · Value Communication Testing · Feature Prioritization Signals.

**Who to ask:** PMs (struggling-adoption features), analytics teams (retention/usage data), then up to Directors and VPs.

## Engineering

**When rework breaks velocity.** Shipping code fast is meaningless if half of it gets rewritten after launch. Every usability issue that slips through design becomes engineering rework.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Top metrics | velocity, rework costs, defect rates |
| Where design creates lift | Catching usability failures before they consume sprints or production cycles |

**Questions to ask:**
- Which flows cause rework after release?
- Where does unclear design create engineering churn?
- Which signals would help de-risk code before it ships?

**How to measure:** Run usability tests before handoff; track completion and error rates.

**Signal chain:**
- error rate in usability testing → defects and support tickets post-launch → engineering velocity and reduced rework costs

**Focus signals:** Can users complete it without error? (task success, error rate) — Do they complete it quickly? (time on task) — Is the flow simple enough? (effort rating).

**Jargon to mirror back:** velocity, defect rate, rework, tech debt, cycle time.
- Instead of "users struggled with the flow," say "this flow will increase defect rate."
- Instead of "let's polish the interface," say "simplifying this step will reduce tech debt."

**Worked example:** Usability test showed a 40% error rate in a key workflow. Fix cut errors to 10%, saving weeks of post-release rework.

**Design-supported concepts:** Pre-Handoff Usability Testing · Error Prevention Design · Prototype Stress-Testing · Pattern Validation · Bug Source Analysis · Workflow Simplification.

**Who to ask:** QA leads (most common bugs), engineering managers (velocity and rework), then up to VPs of Engineering.

## Strategy

**When big bets pay off…or don't.** Every investment is a bet on new markets, products, or differentiators. If those bets fail, growth stalls and competitors pull ahead.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Top metrics | market share, innovation rate, competitive differentiation |
| Where design creates lift | De-risking innovation by proving desirability and differentiation early |

**Questions to ask:**
- What unmet needs are competitors ignoring?
- Which concepts excite users enough to shift perception?
- Which signals would make leadership more confident in their bets?

**How to measure:** Run concept testing, desirability rankings, and preference signals across competitors.

**Signal chain:**
- desirability and preference signals → concept adoption and market feedback → market share and competitive differentiation

**Focus signals:** Do users understand it? (comprehension) — Do they want it? (desirability) — Do they prefer it? (preference comparison).

**Jargon to mirror back:** market share, differentiation, innovation pipeline, risk reduction, vision alignment.
- Instead of "this concept is interesting," say "users preferred this option over competitors by 40%."
- Instead of "this is risky," say "validation shows unmet needs that make this a smart bet."

**Worked example:** New product idea scored only 25% preference against competitor concepts. Refining the value prop raised preference to 60%, giving leadership confidence to move forward.

**Design-supported concepts:** Concept Testing · Unmet Needs Discovery · Competitive Benchmarking · Value Proposition Validation · Innovation Risk Reduction · Vision Alignment Testing.

**Who to ask:** product strategy leads (which ideas need validation), competitive intelligence (market comparisons), then up to executives.

## Operations

**When workflows break, costs pile up.** Every broken onboarding flow, clunky internal tool, or confusing process multiplies support tickets, slows adoption, and drains resources.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Top metrics | efficiency, support costs, internal adoption speed |
| Where design creates lift | Turning design fixes into operational savings and smoother processes |

**Questions to ask:**
- Which workflows generate the most support tickets?
- Where do employees struggle with internal tools?
- Which signals would help prove efficiency gains?

**How to measure:** Track error rates, completion time, and satisfaction for both customer and employee flows.

**Signal chain:**
- error rate and completion in workflows → adoption speed of tools and processes → lower support costs and higher efficiency

**Focus signals:** Can users complete it without error? (completion, error rate) — How long does it take? (time on task) — Do they need extra help? (helpfulness, support reliance).

**Jargon to mirror back:** ticket volume, handle time, adoption rate, first contact resolution (FCR), cost per interaction.
- Instead of "this flow was confusing," say "this step adds 2 minutes to handle time."
- Instead of "users needed help," say "confusion here created 500 tickets last quarter."

**Worked example:** Testing revealed only 50% of new customers could complete onboarding without assistance. Fix raised success to 85% and cut related support tickets by 40%.

**Design-supported concepts:** Onboarding Flow Optimization · Internal Tool Usability · Support Ticket Analysis · Process Simplification · Form and Input Validation · Self-Service Flow Testing.

**Who to ask:** support teams (highest call drivers), ops managers (efficiency and adoption metrics), then up to COOs/VP Ops.

## Finance

**When ROI decides what lives or dies.** Every team fights for budget; Finance decides where resources go. If design cannot show how its work impacts revenue, costs, or efficiency, funding gets cut.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Top metrics | revenue growth, margin, ROI |
| Where design creates lift | Making design ROI visible and defensible |

**Questions to ask:**
- How can design decisions tie to measurable financial impact?
- Where can signals serve as leading indicators for revenue or churn?
- Which metrics matter most for this budget cycle?

**How to measure:** Connect usability and adoption signals directly to retention, churn, or lifetime value.

**Signal chain:**
- completion, satisfaction, error rates → adoption, churn, support tickets → revenue, margin, ROI

**Focus signals:** Do users adopt? (completion, adoption rate) — Do they stay? (retention signals) — Do they struggle? (error rates, effort scores).

**Jargon to mirror back:** ROI, CAC, LTV, margin, churn rate.
- Instead of "users liked it," say "this reduced churn risk by $2M in annual revenue."
- Instead of "this improved clarity," say "support costs dropped 20%."

**Worked example:** Usability fix improved onboarding completion by 30%. Support costs dropped, saving $500k annually, and retention rose, boosting revenue.

**Design-supported concepts:** Adoption-to-Retention Tracking · Support Cost Reduction · Churn Prevention Signals · Efficiency Gains Evidence · ROI Case Studies.

**Who to ask:** FP&A analysts (cost drivers this quarter), Finance managers (link signals to churn/revenue/margin), then up to CFOs.

## Legal

**When risk blocks progress.** Every unclear consent screen, inaccessible flow, or misleading message opens the company to liability. When risk is high, legal slows launches or restricts what design can do.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Top metrics | compliance, liability cost, risk exposure |
| Where design creates lift | Preventing legal exposure by simplifying risky experiences (consent, accessibility) |

**Questions to ask:**
- Where are users confused about consent or terms?
- How does accessibility show up in flows?
- Which flows worry legal about risk exposure?

**How to measure:** Test comprehension of terms, clarity of consent, and accessibility.

**Signal chain:**
- comprehension and accessibility scores → reduced consent errors and accessibility issues → fewer compliance failures and liability costs

**Focus signals:** Do users understand it? (comprehension) — Do they consent correctly? (task success) — Can everyone access it? (accessibility checks).

**Jargon to mirror back:** compliance rate, liability cost, accessibility compliance, regulatory risk, dark patterns.
- Instead of "users were confused," say "confusion here creates regulatory risk."
- Instead of "the terms feel dense," say "comprehension rates are too low for compliance."

**Worked example:** Testing revealed 45% of users misinterpreted a consent checkbox. Redesigning it raised comprehension to 85%, satisfying legal and reducing liability risk.

**Design-supported concepts:** Consent Flow Testing · Accessibility Validation · Plain-Language Terms · Dark Pattern Removal · Compliance-by-Design Checks.

**Who to ask:** compliance officers (highest-risk flows), legal advisors (link signals to user understanding/accessibility), then up to general counsel/VPs.

## Quick Exercise (30 minutes, applies to all eight)

Pick one business function you work with this week.

1. Ask them the questions in their section above.
2. Map one of their top metrics to a design signal.
3. Share back one quick test you can run together.

This proves design belongs in their workflow.

## Quick Test (workflow impact is real if all three are yes)

1. Did users succeed without friction?
2. Did adoption or retention improve?
3. Did it move a metric this function already tracks?

If all three are yes, signals have become proof and workflows show real design impact.
