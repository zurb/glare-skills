# SIGNAL Scoring Model — Reference

Compressed source: Glare | Design Review, Scoring Model v1.3.

## What the scoring model does

Looks at each part of the conversation and measures two things:

1. how clearly the step showed up
2. how much it shaped the direction of the call

This helps teams move beyond "that felt like a good call" and see where the conversation actually gained or lost strength.

## Inputs

Start with a real design review conversation. Best inputs (in order):

1. a call transcript
2. a meeting recording turned into text
3. notes from the review (if no transcript is available)

A transcript works best because it shows what actually happened.

## How scoring works

Each part of SIGNAL is scored from **1 to 5**, independently:

**Surface → Identify → Ground → Navigate → Align → Lock**

After scoring, look for: where scores are low, where clarity first dropped, where the conversation lost strength, what to improve next time.

You do not need to fix everything at once. Improving one or two weak areas can change how the next review performs.

## The section scale

| Score | Level | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Leading | The step is clear, consistent, and actively shapes how the group thinks and decides. |
| 4 | Strong | The step is clear and influences the direction, with minor gaps. |
| 3 | Functional | The step is present and working, but not shaping the conversation in a meaningful way. |
| 2 | Emerging | The step shows up briefly but does not influence the direction. |
| 1 | Reactive | The step is mostly absent. The conversation moves forward without it. |

## Total bands (/30)

| Range | Level | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| 25–30 | Leading | Strong clarity across the full decision flow. The group moved from problem to action with confidence. |
| 19–24 | Advancing | Worked well overall, with a few gaps that softened clarity or momentum. |
| 13–18 | Functional | Had useful moments, but several steps were weak or underdeveloped. |
| 6–12 | Reactive | Lacked structure. Decisions were unclear, weak, or not fully secured. |

**Patterns matter more than totals.** A call can have a decent total and still break down in one important area. Look for the step where clarity first dropped.

## Calibration rules

Use the full scale, but score carefully.

- 1 means the step was mostly absent.
- 2 means the team attempted the step, but it did not shape the call.
- 3 means the step was present and useful.
- 4 means the step was strong, with one or two gaps.
- 5 means the step clearly shaped how the group thought and decided.

Score each section independently. Do not let one strong part lift every score. Do not let one weak part pull everything down.

## Per-section scoring guide

### Surface Challenges

A strong score means the real problem is clear, specific, and grounded in consequence.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Clear pain and consequence surfaced |
| 4 | Tension identified, consequence partially quantified |
| 3 | Friction visible and discussed, but not pushed far enough to shape the call |
| 2 | Surface-level acknowledgment, no depth |
| 1 | Feature-level conversation only |

### Identify Outcomes

A strong score means the group is aligned on what success looks like.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Clear, measurable outcome agreed |
| 4 | Outcomes stated and mostly measurable, agreement implicit |
| 3 | Goals stated but vague |
| 2 | Outcomes mentioned but not defined |
| 1 | Deliverables dominate discussion |

### Ground in Signals

A strong score means the conversation is guided by real signals.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Signals drove the conversation |
| 4 | Signals referenced and influenced key decisions |
| 3 | Some signal grounding |
| 2 | Signals mentioned but not used for decisions |
| 1 | Opinion-driven review |

### Navigate Decisions

A strong score means a clear direction is formed.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Clear recommendation and explicit agreement |
| 4 | Recommendation given, agreement mostly clear |
| 3 | Direction provided but commitment soft |
| 2 | Options presented without recommendation |
| 1 | Discussion without decision |

### Align Ownership

A strong score means responsibility is clear.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Clear ownership and clean boundaries |
| 4 | Most ownership explicit, minor gaps |
| 3 | Partial clarity |
| 2 | Some ownership discussed, not confirmed |
| 1 | Ambiguity persists |

### Lock Momentum

A strong score means the conversation leads directly to action.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 5 | Impact and next steps locked |
| 4 | Next steps defined with owners, timeline soft |
| 3 | Next steps soft |
| 2 | Vague follow-up mentioned |
| 1 | Call ended without commitment |

## Behavioral metrics (supporting evidence)

These are not scored — they explain why sections scored the way they did.

- **Talk Time** — who carried the conversation
- **Problem Ownership** — who named the real tension
- **Decision Language** — words like decide, approve, commit, move forward, next step
- **Signal Use** — whether evidence changed the direction
- **Reframe Adoption** — whether the group picked up a clearer way to understand the problem
- **Closing Clarity** — whether owners, actions, and timing were confirmed

## What the score should produce

Beyond a number:

- total score
- score band
- section scores across Surface → Lock
- short summary of the call pattern
- where clarity first dropped
- strengths to keep using
- weak points to improve
- primary improvement for the next call

## How to use the results

After scoring, look for the first weak point in the flow. That is usually where the next improvement should happen.

- If Surface is weak, clarify the real problem earlier.
- If Identify is weak, define success before debating options.
- If Ground is weak, bring stronger signals into the review.
- If Navigate is weak, make recommendations and tradeoffs more explicit.
- If Align is weak, clarify who owns the next move.
- If Lock is weak, close with clear actions, owners, and timing.

## Primary improvement format

Use this template:

```
Primary improvement: [specific behavior to change]
Why it matters: [how this weakened the call]
Next time: [what to do differently]
```

Example:

```
Primary improvement: Make a clear recommendation.
Why it matters: The group had enough signal to choose a direction, but the conversation stayed open.
Next time: State the recommended direction, explain the tradeoff, and ask for agreement before moving on.
```

## Strengths and improvement areas

**Strengths** show what the team should keep doing. After scoring, identify parts that helped create clarity. Behaviors worth repeating.

**Improvement areas** come directly from the scores and transcript evidence. Focus on moments that weakened clarity, slowed the decision, or made action less certain. Keep this section focused — pick the areas that would most improve the next review, not every weak spot.
