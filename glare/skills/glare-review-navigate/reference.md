# Navigate Decisions — Reference

Compressed source: Glare | Design Review, Navigating Techniques v1.3 + the N row of the Rubric and Scoring Model.

## Intent

Lead the decision process and secure commitment. Move the group from open discussion to a clear path forward.

## Why it matters

Direction without agreement creates rework. If decisions are not secured in the room, they reopen later.

When Navigate is weak:
- conversations stay open too long
- ideas compete without resolution
- tradeoffs are unclear
- people hesitate to commit
- the group leaves without a clear direction

When Navigate is strong, the conversation becomes focused. The group understands what direction to take and why.

## What it considers

- whether a recommendation is made
- whether tradeoffs are clear
- whether agreement begins to form

## What to listen for

- clear recommendations
- acknowledgment of tradeoffs
- movement toward a decision

## What this unlocks

A clear direction gives the group something to align around.

## What breaks if it's weak

Options are discussed but not resolved. Decisions are delayed.

## Connection to the next step

Ownership depends on a clear direction.

## Scoring (1–5)

| Score | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Leading | Clear recommendation and explicit agreement |
| 4 | Advancing | Recommendation given, agreement mostly clear |
| 3 | Functional | Direction provided but commitment soft |
| 2 | Developing | Options presented without recommendation |
| 1 | Reactive | Discussion without decision |

**Metric hint:** Use **Decision Language** — are clients/teammates using commitment language (decide, approve, move forward)?

## When to use Navigate techniques

- the group keeps exploring without choosing
- multiple ideas are treated equally
- people hesitate after a recommendation
- tradeoffs are unclear
- no one wants to close the discussion
- the call is almost over and the direction is still soft

By the end of Navigate, the team should be able to say: what direction is recommended, what tradeoffs are being made, which options are no longer being considered, why this path fits the problem/outcome/signals, whether the group agrees enough to move forward.

## Technique chooser

| If… | Use… |
|---|---|
| The conversation is staying open | State a clear recommendation |
| People hesitate | Frame the tradeoffs |
| Too many ideas are competing | Narrow the options |
| Agreement is assumed | Check for agreement |

## The four techniques

### 1. State a clear recommendation

Without a recommendation, conversations tend to stay open. A recommendation gives the group something to respond to.

- **Watch for:** long discussions without a clear path; multiple ideas being treated equally; uncertainty at the end of the call.
- **What it does:** creates direction; focuses the group; makes decisions easier to form.
- **Example:** "Based on what we've seen, we recommend moving forward with this approach."

### 2. Frame the tradeoffs

Every decision involves tradeoffs. When they aren't stated, people may hesitate or raise concerns later. Framing tradeoffs makes the decision more transparent.

- **Watch for:** hesitation after a recommendation; concerns being raised late; unclear priorities.
- **What it does:** builds trust; clarifies priorities; supports stronger decisions.
- **Example:** "This simplifies the experience, but reduces flexibility here. That's the trade we're making."

### 3. Narrow the options

Too many options slow decision-making. Narrowing helps the group concentrate on the strongest directions and move toward a choice.

- **Watch for:** too many ideas being discussed; difficulty comparing options; stalled decisions.
- **What it does:** simplifies the conversation; improves focus; speeds up decisions.
- **Example:** "Between these two directions, which better solves the problem we outlined?"

### 4. Check for agreement

Agreement is often assumed but not confirmed. People may leave with different interpretations.

- **Watch for:** silence after a recommendation; unclear reactions from the group; decisions being revisited later.
- **What it does:** confirms alignment; prevents rework; strengthens decisions.
- **Example:** "Does this direction make sense to move forward with?"

## Coaching for low Navigate scores

**Common failure modes:**
- Presenting options without a recommendation
- Not asking explicitly for agreement
- Letting "we'll think about it" stand as an outcome

**Behavioral drills:**
- "Based on [signal], we recommend [option] because [reason]. Should we proceed?"
- "The trade-off is ___. Given your priorities, we'd suggest ___."
- "I want to make sure we leave with a decision on this. Are we aligned on ___?"

**Success signal:** Someone uses commitment language ("let's do that," "yes, proceed," "agreed").

## Bottom line

Help the conversation move toward a clear direction. When decisions are guided well, the group stops exploring and starts aligning. The work becomes clearer, and the next step becomes easier to define.
