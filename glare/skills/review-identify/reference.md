# Identify Outcomes — Reference

Compressed source: Glare | Design Review, Identify Techniques v1.3 + the I row of the Rubric and Scoring Model.

## Intent

Define what "better" means in measurable terms. Turn reactions into something more specific: what should improve, what should change for the user, how success will show up.

## When to use

- When the user is defining what "better" means in measurable terms before reacting to feedback.
- When stakeholders critique artifacts because outcomes aren't clear.
- When the team can't pick a direction because success is unclear.
- When the conversation is about deliverables instead of user outcomes.
- When Identify scored 1–2 on the SIGNAL rubric.
- When prepping a review where you need an outcome statement up front.

**Don't use when** the problem itself is still unclear (use `glare:review-surface`) or when the outcome is clear but the team is debating opinion (use `glare:review-ground`).

## Why it matters

Without clarity about outcomes, stakeholders critique artifacts. Clear outcomes shift focus from screens to results.

When Identify is weak:
- feedback is based on preference
- people disagree without a clear way to resolve it
- suggestions pull the conversation in different directions
- decisions feel uncertain or get delayed

When Identify is strong, the conversation becomes easier to guide. People can evaluate ideas against the same goal.

## What it considers

- whether business objectives are explicit
- whether user needs are clear
- whether success is shared and understood

## What to listen for

- clear statements of what should improve
- connections between user behavior and business impact
- agreement on what success looks like

## What this unlocks

Clear outcomes give the conversation a target and help guide decisions.

## What breaks if it's weak

The call drifts into opinions and preferences without a clear way to evaluate them.

## Connection to the next step

Signals need a clear outcome to point toward.

## Scoring (1–5)

| Score | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Leading | Clear, measurable outcome agreed |
| 4 | Advancing | Outcomes stated and mostly measurable, agreement implicit |
| 3 | Functional | Goals stated but vague |
| 2 | Developing | Outcomes mentioned but not defined |
| 1 | Reactive | Deliverables dominate discussion |

## When to use Identify techniques

- feedback is based on preference
- people use words like better, clearer, or easier without defining them
- the group disagrees but cannot resolve the disagreement
- suggestions pile up without a shared goal
- the team has not defined what should change for the user

By the end of Identify, the team should be able to say: what should improve, who it should improve for, what the user should be able to do, how the team will know the direction is working.

## Technique chooser

| If… | Use… |
|---|---|
| Success is unclear | Define what success looks like |
| Feedback comes as scattered suggestions | Turn feedback into an outcome |
| Feedback is too broad | Ask targeted questions |
| Too many goals are competing | Align on a single priority |

## The four techniques

### 1. Define what success looks like

When success is not clearly defined, people rely on their own judgment. That often leads to different opinions and no clear direction.

- **Watch for:** feedback like "this feels better" or "I like this"; disagreement without resolution; shifting ideas of what success means.
- **What it does:** aligns the group on a shared goal; makes feedback easier to evaluate; supports clearer decisions.
- **Example:** "If this works, users should move through this step without hesitation."

### 2. Turn feedback into an outcome

Feedback often comes as suggestions. If left alone, they pull the conversation in different directions. Turning feedback into an outcome reconnects it to the goal — from what to change to why it matters.

- **Watch for:** long lists of suggestions; feedback that feels disconnected; competing ideas.
- **What it does:** filters noise; keeps the conversation focused; helps prioritize feedback.
- **Example:** "So the goal here is to make this easier to understand at first glance?"

### 3. Ask targeted questions

Open-ended questions tend to create open-ended feedback. Targeted questions guide the conversation toward specific parts of the work.

- **Watch for:** broad or unfocused feedback; people jumping between topics; lack of clear direction.
- **What it does:** improves feedback quality; keeps the conversation focused; reduces noise.
- **Examples:** "Does this interaction make sense on mobile?" / "Would you expect to use this here?"

### 4. Align on a single priority

When too many things are discussed at once, the conversation spreads out and slows down. Aligning on a single priority helps the group focus on what matters most right now.

- **Watch for:** too many topics being discussed; shifting focus during the call; difficulty making decisions.
- **What it does:** creates focus; simplifies decisions; improves momentum.
- **Example:** "What's the one thing we need to get right here?"

## Coaching for low Identify scores

**Common failure modes:**
- Discussing deliverables instead of outcomes
- Accepting vague goals ("improve the experience")
- Not separating user outcome from business outcome

**Behavioral drills:**
- "What would success look like in 6 months — what number changes?"
- "If we do this perfectly, what happens for your users? And what happens for the business?"
- "Let me play that back: the outcome we're targeting is ___. Is that right?"

**Success signal:** Someone in the room confirms a measurable outcome in their own words.

## Bottom line

Give the review a target. When success is clear, feedback has something to point toward and decisions become easier to make.

## Failure modes

These are common ways this skill goes wrong in practice:

- **"Better" without numbers.** Outcomes phrased as "cleaner," "simpler," "more delightful" don't tell the team when it lands. Define what the user should be able to *do* and how the team will know it's working.
- **Outcome that's actually a deliverable.** "Ship the new flow" is a deliverable; the outcome is what the new flow enables (e.g., "first-time users complete activation in under 90 seconds").
- **Identifying after Navigating.** Letting the team pick a direction and then back-filling the outcome to match. Identify must precede Navigate or the team optimizes for the wrong target.
- **Outcome statement nobody owns.** Naming a goal without an owner means the goal doesn't survive the room. Tie the outcome to a single person who will be checking it post-launch.
