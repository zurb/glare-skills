# Scaling Influence — Design Assessment Dimension

Compressed from "Glare | Design Assessment, Scaling Influence v1.3" (May 3, 2026).

> "Design work gets weaker when proof does not travel."

## What this dimension measures

How well **design evidence moves beyond the immediate team** and shapes broader decisions. Strong design teams do not keep proof local. They make it visible, trusted, and useful across the organization. The dimension shows whether design impact stays inside the project or helps shape leadership, strategy, and investment.

A team strong in this dimension can clearly answer:

- Who needs to see this evidence?
- What proof matters to each audience?
- How does this signal affect the larger direction?
- Can leaders understand the value quickly?
- Does design evidence shape priorities?
- Does proof support resourcing or investment?
- Does the organization reuse design signals beyond one project?

## Why it matters

Most design teams create more value than the organization can see. They improve experiences, reduce friction, clarify user needs, run tests, shape direction. But too often, that proof stays close to the people who created it.

When that happens:

- leaders only see final outputs
- strong signals stay inside project teams
- proof gets buried in decks or research tools
- design is asked to explain value over and over
- investment decisions happen without design evidence
- strategy forms without enough user signal
- design stays involved in execution but not direction

The work may be valuable, but the influence does not scale.

## Where momentum breaks

Momentum breaks when proof does not move beyond the project. A team has strong evidence, but leadership never sees it. A designer explains a decision well in one review, but the story does not travel to the next meeting. A research finding is useful across teams, but no one packages it in a way others can apply.

You see weak Scaling Influence when:

- design proof stays inside project folders
- leaders ask the same value questions repeatedly
- strong findings do not influence roadmap or funding decisions
- teams struggle to explain design impact in executive language
- designers rely on live presentations to make evidence land
- proof is too detailed for leadership and too disconnected for strategy
- other teams do not know what design has learned
- design is invited late because its evidence is not visible earlier

The issue is not that the proof is weak. It is that **the proof is not traveling in a form others can use.**

## What strong looks like

Strong Scaling Influence makes design evidence easier to spread. The team knows how to shape proof for different audiences. Contributors share signals clearly. Experts connect patterns across teams. Leaders use design evidence to guide strategy, investment, and priorities.

Strong teams:

- package signals so others can understand them quickly
- connect design proof to goals leaders care about
- share evidence beyond the immediate project
- create repeatable formats for design impact
- turn local findings into broader patterns
- help other teams reuse design signals
- use proof to shape roadmap and investment conversations
- build trust in design as a source of decision clarity

This does not mean turning every finding into an executive story. It means **the right evidence reaches the right people in a form they can use.**

## What weak looks like

Weak Scaling Influence keeps design value trapped. The team may produce strong work, but the proof does not spread. Evidence depends on the person presenting it. Leaders may appreciate design, but they do not consistently use design signals to make bigger decisions.

Weak teams often:

- share findings only with the immediate project team
- create proof that is too detailed for leaders to use
- fail to connect signals to strategic priorities
- rely on designers to explain the work live
- lack a shared format for showing design impact
- miss chances to influence roadmap or investment decisions
- repeat the same value story in different meetings
- struggle to make design evidence trusted outside the team

This makes design feel local, even when the work has larger value.

## How it is evaluated — the three levels

Scaling Influence looks at how design proof moves through the organization. The assessment focuses on three levels — three audiences who use evidence in different ways:

```
Contributors → Experts → Leaders
```

These show whether design evidence can move from local work to shared practice to strategic influence.

### Level 1 — Contributors

The people **closest to the work**. Designers, researchers, product managers, engineers, marketers, and others capturing signals in their own lane. Contributors create the first layer of proof by documenting what they learned and why it matters.

- **Strong:** people can explain the evidence behind their work without relying on opinion or craft alone.
- **Weak:** proof stays in individual heads, files, or project conversations. The work may be thoughtful, but it is hard for others to reuse.

> Contributors make proof **visible at the source.**

### Level 2 — Experts

The people who **connect evidence across teams**. Research ops, design leads, principal designers, UX leaders, data partners, and specialists who create shared methods, standards, formats, and interpretation. Experts turn local signals into patterns others can trust.

- **Strong:** the organization has shared ways to collect, compare, and explain design evidence.
- **Weak:** every team tells the story differently. Evidence becomes hard to compare, and leaders struggle to know what to trust.

> Experts make proof **consistent.**

### Level 3 — Leaders

The people who **use design evidence to shape larger decisions**. Design leaders, product leaders, executives, portfolio owners, and other decision-makers who use signals to guide strategy, priorities, funding, staffing, and roadmap choices.

- **Strong:** design evidence shows up in the conversations that decide what the organization does next.
- **Weak:** design is respected but not always used to steer direction. Leaders may value the work, but decisions still happen without clear user proof.

> Leaders make proof **consequential.**

## How to strengthen Scaling Influence

Start by making design proof easier for others to use. You do not need a new reporting system or a larger communications plan. Start with one strong signal or project and shape it for the next audience that needs to act on it.

The goal is to create a clearer influence path:

```
Contributor → Expert → Leader
```

That path should show what was learned, how it connects to a larger pattern, and why it matters to the decisions the organization needs to make.

**Useful moves:**

- summarize one signal in plain language
- connect the signal to a user need or business goal
- turn local findings into a reusable proof story
- create a simple format for sharing design impact
- identify who needs the evidence next
- explain why the evidence matters to that audience
- connect proof to a roadmap, strategy, or investment decision
- standardize how teams share signals
- bring design evidence into leadership conversations earlier

The goal is not to promote design more loudly. It is to make design evidence easier to **trust, share, and act on.**

## Diagnostic questions

Use these to evaluate how well your team scales influence:

- Who needs to see this proof?
- What decision could this evidence shape?
- Is the signal easy to understand outside the team?
- Can the finding be reused by another team?
- Does this proof connect to a larger pattern?
- Does leadership see why this matters?
- Is design evidence showing up early enough?
- What would make this proof easier to trust?

## What improves when this is strong

When Scaling Influence is strong, design becomes easier to lead with:

- teams spend less time justifying design after the fact
- leaders see proof earlier
- evidence travels across teams
- design becomes part of the organization's decision system, not just its delivery system
- design proof becomes more visible
- trust with leadership grows
- local work connects to larger priorities
- signals get reused across teams
- roadmap and investment decisions get shaped by design
- repeated value explanations reduce
- design's role in strategy increases

This is where design maturity becomes organizational influence.

## How this dimension supports the others

When design influence scales, teams can:

- **organize work** so evidence is easier to reuse
- **manage complexity** with clearer alignment
- **build proof** that leaders can understand
- **guide decisions** that carry beyond one project

## Smallest-leverage next move

Pick one recent signal, finding, or result. Trace the influence path: Contributor → Expert → Leader. Find the weakest link. Can someone see what was learned, how it connects to a larger pattern, and what decision it should influence?

Even simpler: pick **one** strong signal you already have. Identify **one** leader who is about to make a decision that signal could inform. Shape the signal in their language, on one page, connected to their decision. Send it before the meeting, not after.

Do **not** start by building a comms strategy or design report dashboard. Start with one signal, one audience, one decision.

## Note on terminology

Scaling Influence uses Contributors / Experts / Leaders — three audiences, not the Decision Map's Define / Measure / Focus / Lead phases. (The Decision Map's "Show" phase was renamed to "Lead" in v1.3.) The Decision Map describes the operating cycle of the work; Scaling Influence describes who consumes the evidence the work produces.
