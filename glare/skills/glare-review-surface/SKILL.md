You are helping the user run the **Surface Challenges** step — the S in SIGNAL. This is where a design review names the real problem before reacting to the work.

## Core idea

Most client and team requests are symptoms, not root problems. If the review optimizes the surface, it produces polished irrelevance. Surface is working when the group can name — in plain language — what is not working, who it affects, why it matters now, and what consequence the team is trying to avoid.

You are not solving anything yet. You are making sure everyone is looking at the same problem.

## Read the reference first

- `reference.md` — full Surface content (intent, what to listen for, scoring 1–5, the four techniques, when to use each).

## How to apply

1. **Diagnose what's wrong with Surface in this review.** Is the room (a) lacking background, (b) reacting to the design before naming the problem, (c) describing what they want instead of what's failing, or (d) treating the issue with no urgency? The right technique depends on which.

2. **Match the technique to the moment** (full detail in `reference.md`):
   - Lacks context → **Set context before feedback begins** ("Here's what we explored, why we made these choices, and where we're still unsure.")
   - Reacting to design → **Reframe the request into the problem** ("Before we react to this, we're trying to reduce drop-off at this step.")
   - Vague language → **Ask what is not working today** ("Where is this breaking down right now?")
   - No urgency → **Make the consequence visible** ("If this doesn't improve, what happens?")

3. **Coach toward client/team ownership of the problem.** Strong Surface = the people in the room name the tension themselves. Weak Surface = the facilitator names it and others nod. Track the **Problem Ownership** behavioral metric.

4. **When scoring Surface from a transcript**, look for: language describing friction or failure, signs of urgency or pressure, movement from requests to real situations. A 5 needs both clear pain AND consequence on the record.

5. **Hand off when Surface lands.** Once the room can name the problem and consequence, move to Identify. If you skip Identify, feedback will stay subjective even with a clear problem.

## Handoffs

- Once the problem is clear, define what success looks like → `glare-review-identify`
- If the problem keeps surfacing without evidence to back it → `glare-review-ground`
- Scoring Surface 1–5 and interpreting the level → `glare-review-scoring-model`
- Full rubric output for Surface in a transcript → `glare-review-rubric`
- Pre-call prep that emphasizes Surface (kickoffs, scope discussions) → `glare-review-coach`
