## Glare | Measure, Hunches v1.0

Hunches are informed guesses — testable bets that turn instinct, early signs, and experience into measurable hypotheses.

- **What a hunch is**: An informed guess; a shaped instinct about what might be true. *"We believe there is something important here, and we are ready to test it."*
- A strong hunch is: tied to a user problem/friction point; expressed as a clear, provable hypothesis; linked to a metric so results are visible; **falsifiable** — could be wrong, and that's the point.
- **Ideation context**: Ideation is the widest, messiest creative space. Hunches narrow it ("we believe…"); Questions sharpen ("how can we prove this?"); the overlap is where evidence forms.
- **STEP 1 — Start with Context**: Tie the hunch to a business goal, design principle, or user-journey moment. Ask what you're designing, who for, and what you already know. Make hunch-building shared (standups, sketch sessions, research reviews). First hunch won't be the last.
- **STEP 2 — Capture Initial Assumptions**. Spark techniques: Survey Data, Ideation Sketching, Empathy Mapping, Customer Journey Framework, Stakeholder Interviews, Customer Feedback, Data Analytics, Competitive Analysis, Expert Audit.
- **STEP 3 — Spot Opportunities**: Focus on the problem, not the solution. Ask what feels clunky or confusing, where unmet needs exist, where you can add value or reduce effort.
- **STEP 4 — Form Your Hunch**. Belief statement: *"We believe users need ___ so they can ___."* Then design hypothesis: *"We believe that [this change] for [this group] will [have this impact] because [supporting reason]."*
  - Weak: "We believe users like shorter forms."
  - Stronger: "We believe removing two steps from the signup form for first-time users will increase completion rates because users drop off at step 3."
- **STEP 5 — Translate Into Questions**: What behavior would confirm/disprove the hunch? What needs to happen for it to be true? How will we know? Link to a UX metric (completion rate, task time, drop-off). Treat results as signals, not final answers.
- **Benefits of using hunches**: helps focus; avoids building the wrong thing; gets team aligned early; reduces documentation overhead; builds better features grounded in early user signals; keeps a learning loop alive post-launch.
- **How Glare helps**: Glare turns hunches into focused, testable questions so teams move fast without waiting for full research, bridging creative thinking and user-centered decisions.

## Key terms (Hunches-relevant)

- **Hunch** — An informed, falsifiable guess expressed as a hypothesis. Template: *"We believe that [change] for [audience] will [impact] because [reason]."*
- **Hypothesis** — A structured, testable form of a hunch tied to a measurable outcome.
- **Falsifiable** — A hunch/hypothesis property: it could be proven wrong, which is the point.
