## Why hunches matter

Teams get stuck when they wait for perfect data or leap straight to solutions. Both waste time. Hunches cut through by giving you a place to start — they are not answers, they are testable bets. They turn instincts, early signs, and experience into hypotheses tied to signals you can measure.

Without hunches, teams drift toward generic metrics that miss what users actually need. With hunches, you focus on what matters and move fast enough to keep momentum alive.

## What a hunch is

A hunch is an informed guess — a shaped instinct about what might be true. It is the spark that moves you from wide-open ideation into focused exploration.

Think of it as: *"We believe there is something important here, and we are ready to test it."*

A strong hunch is:

- Tied to a user problem or friction point
- Expressed as a clear hypothesis you can prove or disprove
- Linked to a metric so results are visible
- **Falsifiable** — meaning it could be wrong, and that is the point

---

## Ideation framing

Ideation is the widest space, where creative exploration begins. It is open, messy, and unfiltered. Possibilities are still loose, but from this wide field, patterns begin to take shape. The first edges of clarity emerge through **hunches and questions**.

- **Hunch** — A shaped idea worth exploring. An informed guess drawn from experience, observation, or early signs. A hunch narrows wide-open ideation into something testable by suggesting what feels meaningful. *Think of it as: "We believe there is something important here."*
- **Questions** — Sharpen hunches into measurable direction. They expose what needs to be tested, clarify assumptions, and guide teams toward signals that matter. *Think of it as: "How can we prove this is true?"*
- **The overlap** — When a hunch meets a question, ideation becomes focused. Assumptions and opportunities turn into testable paths. The team moves from "we believe" to "let's find out." This overlap is where evidence begins to form and where signals start to emerge.

## How to create a hunch — the 5-step process

### STEP 1 — Start with Context

Tie your hunch to a business goal, design principle, or moment in the user journey. Forming a hunch together helps uncover different perspectives — don't rush. Discuss what each person sees as the real problem and where the opportunity lies.

Ask:

- What are we designing? Who is it for?
- What do we already know from research or experience?

**Example:** *"We believe simplifying the onboarding screen will improve activation by reducing cognitive load."*

Use hunches to be intentional about where you focus and why. Every decision you explore should help you learn something new or confirm what you believe to be true. Your first hunch won't be your last — what you learn may shape a new hunch, and that's how design gets smarter over time. Make hunch-building a shared activity (standups, sketch sessions, research reviews).

### STEP 2 — Capture Initial Assumptions

Hunches don't come out of nowhere — they're shaped by what you've already seen, heard, and explored.

- What do you think users want or struggle with?
- What behaviors or actions do you believe are critical?

**Spark techniques:**

- **Survey Data** — Ask users directly for quick feedback on what's working. Surveys collecting attitudinal and behavioral metrics can be run directly in Helio as leading indicators — giving your team early signal before committing to a direction.
- **Ideation Sketching** — Draw ideas together as a team to turn quick thoughts into early concepts.
- **Empathy Mapping** — Understand what users think, feel, say, and do to uncover hidden needs or frustrations.
- **Customer Journey Framework** — Spot pain points and opportunities as users move through each step of an experience.
- **Stakeholder Interviews** — Learn what internal teams care about or expect — helping you shape useful, aligned ideas.
- **Customer Feedback** — Look at reviews, support tickets, or survey responses to see where people struggle or succeed.
- **Data Analytics** — Use analytics to notice patterns in behavior (drop-offs, clicks, skipped actions).
- **Competitive Analysis** — See how others solve similar problems, and spot gaps or opportunities in your own experience.
- **Expert Audit** — Use the experience of design, product, or research leads to highlight areas worth testing.

### STEP 3 — Spot Opportunities

Focus your hunch on the problem, not the solution. A strong hunch starts with user pain, not a new feature idea.

Ask:

- What feels clunky or confusing in the experience?
- Are there unmet user needs or points of frustration?
- Is there potential to add value or reduce effort?

A strong hunch helps you resist the urge to build more — it sharpens your focus to explore the changes that matter most.

### STEP 4 — Form Your Hunch

A hunch is your early guess, an informed instinct about what users need or where things could be better. It's based on patterns you've seen, behaviors you've observed, or feedback you've gathered.

Start with a simple belief statement:

> **We believe users need ___ so they can ___.**

Then promote that into a structured design hypothesis:

> **We believe that [this change] for [this group] will [have this impact] because [supporting reason].**

**Example:**

- **Hunch:** We believe users need fewer steps so they can complete signup more easily.
- **Design Hypothesis:** We believe that removing two steps from the signup flow for new users will increase completion rates because analytics show drop-offs after step three.

A strong hypothesis:

- Is focused on solving a user problem
- Makes a clear prediction
- Can be tested and measured
- Is falsifiable (it could be wrong, and that's okay)

**Weak vs. strong:**

- Weak: "We believe users like shorter forms."
- Stronger: "We believe removing two steps from the signup form for first-time users will increase completion rates because users drop off at step 3."

### STEP 5 — Translate Into Questions

Turn your hunch into questions that guide what you'll test or learn. Good questions focus on user behavior or outcomes, not opinions.

Ask:

- What behavior would confirm or disprove this hunch?
- What would need to happen for this idea to be true?
- How will we know if the change made a difference?

**Examples:**

- Do users complete signup when fewer steps are shown?
- Does adding auto-save reduce form abandonment?
- Are users more likely to return if we show progress indicators?

Link your hunch to a UX metric — completion rate, task time, drop-off rate. If your hunch connects to an attitudinal or behavioral metric, collect it in Helio as a leading indicator before waiting on performance data. Refer to the UX Metrics documentation to confirm which metrics are Helio-collectible and which require post-launch analytics.

After testing, reflect — did the results support or challenge your thinking? What did you learn? What should you keep, change, or explore next? Test results aren't final answers — they're signals.

## 6 Benefits of Using Hunches

1. **Helps you focus** — Hunches help you figure out what to work on first. They give you a clear place to start based on what seems most important or promising.
2. **Avoid building the wrong thing** — Hunches help teams test early ideas before investing in full solutions, reducing the risk of wasted time and effort.
3. **Everyone's on the same page** — When the team shares thoughts early, it's easier to agree on what to explore. You don't need to convince others later — everyone's already included from the start.
4. **Less documentation** — Since everyone is involved, there's no need for long documents to explain your thinking. The team already understands the direction.
5. **Builds better features** — Because hunches are based on early signs of what users need, you're more likely to create features that are actually helpful and solve real problems.
6. **Keeps you learning** — Even after you launch something, hunches can lead to new ideas and improvements. You keep testing and learning instead of guessing what might work.

## How Glare helps

In early design stages, teams often face uncertainty and lack clear direction. Waiting for complete data can slow progress; moving ahead without focus can lead to wasted time and poor results. Hunches offer a way forward — educated guesses based on instinct, experience, or early insights.

Hunches work best when they're part of your team's regular rhythm — revisited in retros, research reviews, and iteration planning. Glare helps teams turn these hunches into focused questions they can test. Instead of waiting for full research, teams use hunches to identify opportunities, prioritize what to explore, and move quickly.

Hunches help teams stay flexible, learn faster, and design with greater impact — even when the path isn't fully clear.

## Key terms (Hunches-relevant)

- **Hunch** — An informed, falsifiable guess expressed as a hypothesis. Template: *"We believe that [change] for [audience] will [impact] because [reason]."*
- **Hypothesis** — A structured, testable form of a hunch tied to a measurable outcome.
- **Falsifiable** — A hunch/hypothesis property: it could be proven wrong, which is the point.
- **Ideation** — The widest creative space; hunches and questions emerge from it. When a hunch meets a question, ideation becomes focused.
